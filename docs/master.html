DAWN OF DECAY — Master Design Document

The single authoritative reference for what is CURRENTLY in the game — it describes the present build only (the timestamp above is bumped on every edit); the history of changes lives in the changelog. Engine: Godot 4.7, all UI built in code.

1. Concept

A turn-based party roguelike. The world is being consumed by the Decay; the player commands the Untouched — four heroes immune to it — through zone after zone of corrupted enemies. Runs are short, buildcrafted, and repeatable: draft a party, awaken permanent specializations, walk a road of fixed encounters, defeat the zone boss, descend deeper. Death ends the run but feeds a permanent relic collection (meta-progression). Combat is timeline-initiative with a timing skill check on every action — the player's execution matters as much as their build.

2. Run Flow

Main Menu → Draft (pick 4 heroes + equip up to 3 relics) → Spec Choice (permanent; heroes begin with no runes and three empty slots) → Map (the road) → Offer (three bargains, before every elite and mini-boss) → Battle → sometimes Shop and/or ??? → back to the map → … → Mini-boss → … → Zone Boss → next zone (party fully restored) → end boss → run complete. Talents, runes and picks are all handled on the map's hero cards; there is no separate Party screen.

A run ends in one of three ways: completion (the final boss falls), a wipe (every hero down), or a forfeit — the player abandons the run from the battle burger menu, behind a confirmation. A forfeit ends the run exactly as a wipe does (save cleared, party reset), so it carries no advantage; the player picks a reason from a short list, and it is recorded as a forfeit rather than a defeat everywhere it is counted. All three route into the same run summary (§11a).

  • Main menu: New Game, Continue (enabled when a save exists), Relics (collection viewer), Settings (master volume, fullscreen — persisted to user://settings.cfg), Exit.
  • Draft: heroes shown as sprite cards; click to select. Selection order = battle position (slot order also sets each hero's tint). One of each class — picking a duplicate moves it. Up to 3 unlocked relics may be equipped for the run. The Road picks the run's difficulty: Standard (the game as designed) or Wanderer (enemies at 70% strength — an alpha testing aid so a tester can see every zone; saved with the run).
  • Spec choice: offered per hero after the first victory. Three cards per class, each showing the spec name, its archetype in bold (mouseover for the archetype explanation), blurb, passive, and full ability details. The choice is permanent for the run.
  • Victory rewards: gold for every fight, and clearing any encounter heals the party 15% of max HP — there is no resting, so this is the whole recovery curve (the Chalice of Dawn relic stacks its 10% on top for 25%). Talent points come from the beats that matter — 1 for confirming a spec, then 1 apiece from every elite, mini-boss and boss, the final boss included (§7). Regular fights award none. The mini-boss hands every hero a generic ability upgrade, three offered; zone bosses 1 and 2 hand every hero an ability pick, three offered, drawn from that hero's spec pool only (§6a). The final boss awards a relic unlock and big gold and ends the run — no ability pick, because nothing follows it to use one on. Fallen heroes return after a won battle at 20% max HP ("the Untouched refuse to stay down").
  • Defeat: the run ends, the save is cleared, back to a new draft.
  • Saving: auto-save to user://run_save.bin (format v7) after every completed encounter AND after spec confirmation (including debug rerolls); Continue resumes at the map. Loading swaps each member's saved tree snapshot for the live definition, so balance edits reach a run in flight, and refunds points sunk into nodes that no longer exist. A save older than v7 describes a branching board this build cannot walk, so it is refused and cleared rather than half-loaded. Run end (win or wipe) clears the save.

Zone 3's boss is the end boss: the run ends on its death. There is no route to plan — the only forward move is the next encounter — so the run's decisions live on the offer screen (§3a) rather than in routing.

The road reads LEFT TO RIGHT: encounter 1 sits on the left edge and the boss on the right. Each encounter is a dot on the line; the current position is marked, cleared dots dim, and dots ahead show their type by colour. Hovering a dot scouts it — theme, full lineup, and the band's damage-type identity ("Resists: Nature x3 / Soft to: Fire x2"), from the moment the zone is generated. Mini-boss and boss dots show their type but not their identity: the player learns which boss they drew only by fighting the mini-boss.

The mini-boss holds the middle of every zone. Encounter 6 is an elite warband carrying a boss-tier health pool (every enemy at 1.5× its usual maximum, attack untouched — a longer fight, not a harder-hitting one). It pays elite gold (80–100) and 1 talent point, and its own spoil is a generic ability upgrade (§6a). The offer precedes it (§3a), so a mini-boss is fought under a modifier — with every enemy already at 1.5× health a severity-4 pick is a real gamble, and the guaranteed low-severity option is what makes it a choice rather than a trap. Its spoils stack with the bargain's reward. Bosses remain fixed encounters: no modifier, no offer.

Elites are the snowball engine. An elite kill pays 1 talent point, drops a rune cache for a random hero (three candidates rolled at the kill, one chosen on that hero's card), plus a random consumable and a bigger gold purse (80–100).

3a. The offer — three bargains

Before every elite and mini-boss encounter — not plain fights, not bosses — the player is shown 3 options and picks one. That is four a zone: the bargain is an event on the road, not a gate in front of every battle. Each option is one modifier plus one reward, both visible before choosing. There is no decline.

A modifier is a condition that binds both parties for that battle. Each carries a severity of 1–4, and the reward table reads that severity — so authoring a modifier means authoring one number, not a pairing. Severity is flat, authored per modifier, and deliberately ignores party composition: a modifier that happens to be cheap for your build is a good deal you earned by building that way. A modifier binds every combatant on the field, including a companion summoned mid-battle.

How the three cards are dealt. The first comes from the severity 1–2 pool and the other two from the severity 3–4 pool, so every offer reads the same way: one safe option, two gambles. The floor — at least one option of severity 1 or 2 — is therefore guaranteed by construction rather than by rejecting rolls, and a wounded party is never cornered.

The reward pays on victory: the modifier is the price, and the reward is what clearing the fight under it bought.

The pool is 20, weighted low. Because the safe slot on every card is served by the 1–2 pool alone, that is the end a run actually sees repeatedly — so it is the end that is authored deepest.

3b. What follows an encounter

Shops and events are scheduled now, not placed on a board. Both roll once, on the victory screen, after any cleared fight or elite:

  • Merchant — 40%, with a floor: if 4 encounters pass with no merchant, the next cleared one guarantees a merchant. The severity-4 reward option guarantees one on demand. Shop contents are unchanged — consumables at existing prices, one rune offer per hero per visit, duplicates avoided.
  • Event — 25%, drawn from the existing pool with its requirement gating intact.

When both roll, the merchant resolves first, then the event, then the map.

Events: an event presents flavor text and 2+ choices; choices whose requirements fail are greyed with the reason. Effects speak a fixed verb vocabulary — gold / gold_pct / heal_pct / damage_pct (never kills: floors at 1 HP) / mana_pct / max_hp_pct / attack_pct (permanent for the run, applied at battle spawn) / talent_points / item / random_item / revive_pct (the fallen return) / relic_grant (random inactive relic; +40 gold when slots are full) — aimed by target selectors (party, random, lowest_hp, class:<key>) and gated by requirement conditions (min_gold, max_gold, zone_slot, has_item, spec_in_party, fallen_hero). Adding an event is a JSON edit; adding a verb is one handler. 16 events ship now (three zone-gated, two spec-gated, one needing a fallen hero); the schema is built for 40+. Seen events persist in the save and never repeat within a run until the pool exhausts.

3c. Attrition and potions

There is no resting. Clearing any encounter heals the party 15% of max HP (the Chalice of Dawn relic's 10% stacks on top). Potions are usable on the map screen, not only in battle: the inventory is interactive and targets a hero from the stat cards. The Bomb is battle-only (nothing on the road to throw it at) and the Defense Potion is battle-only (its 3 turns mean nothing outside a fight); both say so on hover. Potion cap: 6 per item type — purchases and drops above the cap are refused with a message rather than silently lost, and the shop greys a full stack rather than taking the gold.

  • Zones — authored slots: a run is three zone slots, and each slot draws one zone from its own authored candidate pool — zones are designed FOR a position (openers are not finales), so a zone def carries its name, its boss, and a roster per slot it can host. The current pools run fixed: 1. Forest of Old (roster 1) → 2. The Scarlands (roster 2) → 3. Forest of Old as the finale (tougher roster 3 — behemoths walk the old woods). Random slot draws sit behind the DOD_ZONE_ROTATION=1 flag until each pool holds ~2 candidates; every zone keeps the same 12-encounter shape so the talent economy never shifts. Deeper-slot enemies are tinted redder, and the slot sets the stat multiplier (§4.3). The finale's boss — the Withered Warden again, by design — is the FINAL boss: the run ends on its death.
  • Zone completion: killing zone 1's or zone 2's boss awards a relic unlock, gold, 1 talent point, an ability pick for every hero (§6a), and fully restores the party (HP and Mana) before the descent.
  • Map screen: zone art background, the road with a position readout ("Zone 2 of 3 — encounter 7 of 12 (19 of 36) — the Ash Tyrant waits"), and all four hero stat cards. Each card carries portrait, spec name, HP bar with numbers, resource bar, three rune slots (filled or empty — click one to open that hero's pouch), an ◆N ability-upgrade count alongside them (hidden at zero; hovering it lists them as "Overpower — Honed", one per line — it takes no click of its own), and an unspent-talent badge; clicking a card opens that hero's talent tree and full sheet. Owed ability picks, ability upgrades and rune picks are resolved on the card itself. Also on screen: gold, the interactive potion inventory (§3c), and the burger menu (Restart Run / Glossary / Quit to Main Menu / Quit to Desktop). There is no Party button and no separate Party screen. The cleared stretch of road is lit gold, cleared dots are dim green, the next dot wears its full type colour and is the only one clickable, everything ahead recedes grey.

4. Combat System

4.1 Initiative timeline

Every unit sits on a shared timeline. After acting, a unit's next turn arrives after delay × 100 / effective speed time units (delay is the ability's initiative cost, listed in every tooltip; basic actions are 2.0, heavy abilities up to 6.0). Speed 100 is average. Effective speed is what the timeline reads, everywhere — the opening roll that seeds the first turn order as well as every reschedule after it — so every modifier to speed genuinely bends the clock. Slow reduces it to 75%; Chilled to 75% at one stack and 50% at two or more, deeper still under Frigid Grip; Quick Draw raises it by 50%. Some abilities shove the target down the timeline (delay_push). The initiative bar (top of screen, 14 slots, 38×53 portraits) always shows the coming order — with one exception: an enemy held in a Cryomancer's Glacial Hold is removed from the bar entirely, because it is not going to act. Hovering any individual ability re-sorts the bar with a gold "ghost" portrait showing where that cast would land your next turn. The ghost locks in when the ability is chosen and persists through targeting and the skill check.

4.2 Skill checks

Every hero action runs a timing bar: a cursor sweeps 0→1→0 (0.72s per sweep); stop it with Spacebar or Left Click.

Perfect bonuses buy RELIABILITY, not magnitude, wherever an ability already carries a chance roll. On those the Perfect does not add damage or a hit — it removes the roll: Wildstrikes and Hack and Slash land their Bleed on every strike, Blizzard puts 2 stacks of Chilled on every enemy instead of 1–2, Firestorm spreads its bolts evenly across the living instead of scattering them, Arcane Barrage never strikes the same enemy twice while an unstruck one stands, Triple Shot pays out one guaranteed critical, and Pommel Strike and a perfectly rigged Snare Trap land their Stun even on an unbroken boss. Abilities with no roll to remove keep an ordinary bonus — a perfect Deadfall gets a fourth spring.

4.3 Attack resolution pipeline

Per strike, in order: ability % × the attacker's current Attack × random variance (0.9–1.1) × grade multiplier × crit (×1.5; the Sharpshooter's Lethal Aim ×2, rising with Focus) × attacker modifiers × target modifiers × (1 − resist) × (1 − effective armor), minimum 1.

  • Attack (stat) & node scaling: every unit has an Attack stat, and every ability's damage is a PERCENT of the user's current Attack (a "45%" ability at 100 Attack hits for ~45). Base Attack comes from the spec's role — Damage 100, Tank 75, Support 50 (per-spec stat blocks are being filled in class by class). All scaling is LINEAR on the base value: heroes gain +2% of base Attack and +2% of base max HP per combat node won (fight/elite/boss); enemy scaling is zone-local — each zone replays its own tier ladder at +2% of base Attack and +2.5% of base HP per tier (1–11), multiplied by the zone's slot in the run: 1st zone ×1.0, 2nd ×1.5, 3rd ×2.2 (keyed by position, not identity — the Forest of Old is ×1.0 as the opener and ×2.2 as the finale; slots past the third continue the ~×1.5 step, so adding zones never touches the formula). Armor, Resistances, Speed, Constitution, Crit, Block, and Parry never scale. Damage-over-time effects (Burn 6%, Poison 3% per stack) snapshot the applier's Attack when they land. Battle tooltips show the computed damage numbers; the hero stat page shows the numbers AND the % scaling behind them.
  • Mitigation is itemized in the log: every landed hit reports what the defenses ate — "(nature resist -8)" for resistances, "(armor -12)" for armor, and "(WEAK! +9)" when a Weakness ADDED damage — alongside the parry/block source tags.
  • Resistances (stat): heroes carry a resistance per element — Fire, Frost, Nature, Holy, Shadow, Arcane — working exactly like armor but against that damage type (negative = weakness). Hover "Resistances" on the stat page to read them; per-spec values are being assigned class by class (all 0% until then).
  • Miss: 5% base chance (single-target, non-counter only); a Dazed attacker is 20% more likely to miss, a Blinded one 50% more. Attacks against an Elusive target (Canis/Aguila) are 25% more likely to miss. AoE never misses/parries. Multi-hit and random-hit attacks roll miss per strike — a missed hit never cancels the follow-up strikes.
  • Parry: only MELEE attacks can be parried — ranged attackers (Hunter and Mage among heroes; Orc Archers and Shamans among enemies) sail past the blade. Baseline is split by side — heroes parry at 5%, enemies at 2.5% — plus parry bonuses (talents, Parry Up). A spec's stat block can replace the hero baseline outright: the Swordmaster parries at 12%, the game's only parry-STAT character, exactly as the Warden is its only Block-stat one. A parried hit still lands but with 75% less damage AND 75% less Break damage (on-hit statuses can still apply); the log names the parry's source — reflexes, Sword Mastery, or Parry Up — e.g. "(parried -75% — Sword Mastery)". Parrying does not trigger an automatic counter. AoE cannot be parried; Broken units cannot parry; multi-hit attacks roll parry per strike.
  • Counter Attack (term): immediately answering a parry — a free action with no initiative cost that cannot chain. Granted only by specific effects, all of them the Swordmaster's: Riposte and Opportunist both answer a parry with a free Overpower (a parry fires ONE counter even if he holds both), and the Untouchable capstone answers with a free Pommel Strike instead while he is in the Defensive stance. Opportunist also answers an enemy attack that MISSES.
  • Block (keyword): fully negates an incoming attack — 100% of the damage, the Break damage, and its on-hit effects. Governed by Block %, a SPEC STAT only pure tanks carry (currently the Warden: 10% base, +15% from Heavy Plating, plus Heavy Plating's climbing bonus — +8% per unblocked attack, cap +40%, reset whenever a Block lands; the Orc Shieldmaster also blocks at 5%). The Warden's Shieldwall adds a further +25% while its stance holds, on the Heavy Plating slice of the roll. Guaranteed blocks come from Interpose charges alone — one charge, one blocked attack. Broken units cannot Block. A block flashes BLOCK and the log names its source — "(Interpose)", "(Heavy Plating)", "(Bulwark Line)", or "(base Block)" — which matters: the Warden's Tenacity and Rally talents feed on Heavy Plating blocks specifically, and so, therefore, do the blocks Shieldwall buys.
  • Armor Penetration (keyword): every % of armor penetration negates a % of the target's armor (currently granted only by the Berserker's Crushing Blows talent). It is NOT shown on the character screen.
  • Adjacent (keyword): the enemies DIRECTLY next to a target in formation order, one on each side (never the target itself). A dead neighbor NEGATES the adjacent bonus on that side — the effect does not jump past a corpse to the next living enemy. Splash effects that say "Adjacent" (the Warden's Sundering) hit at most those two enemies.
  • Cooldown (keyword, "cd"): the number of the user's turns before the same ability can be used again — "6cd" means unusable for 6 turns after use. Typical bands: basics 0, light hits 1–2, heavies/AoE 2–5, Resurrection 3. The remaining cooldown shows on the ability button ("CD n") and in its tooltip. Enemies do not use cooldowns at all — their kits are gated by resources and AI conditions instead.
  • Buff / Debuff (keywords): a DEBUFF is any negative status effect, a BUFF any positive one. Talents that count debuffs (Dominant Presence, Iron Will) use this classification.
  • Crit: 10% base + 25% vs Broken targets + 1% per Arcanist Resonance stack + talent/spec bonuses (+15% with Aguila summoned). Guaranteed: Ice Lance vs Frozen; a perfect Execute. Resonance builds only on the Mage's own casts — parry counters do not grant stacks.
  • Attacker modifiers: Resonance +1.5% × N(N+1)/2 at N stacks (a COMPOUNDING curve, not a per-stack slope — see §5.1); Surge +20%; Empower +25%; Cripple −25%; Blood Frenzy up to +40% as HP falls; Seasoned Fighter +15% above half HP; relic bonuses (global +10% dragonbone; fire/holy +20% emberheart); Powershot +2% per 1% of the target's EMPTY Break bar; Overpower +0.5 per point of target Break; Volatility +30% on Arcane Cannon; Fireball perfects at 25% of Attack. Neither Arcane Cannon nor Magi's Wrath carries a per-stack damage term — the passive compounds, and an ability-side per-stack multiplier on top of it would square the escalation. Both keep their Break terms, which ride stacks on a different axis.
  • Target modifiers: Exposed +15%; Seasoned Fighter −15% at or below half HP; Arcanist Resonance +0.75% × N(N+1)/2 damage taken at N stacks — the same compounding curve at half the step, and uncapped (the glass-cannon risk, and the whole bargain); Stabilized −10% per stack consumed.
  • Armor: flat % damage reduction. Fortify +10%; Broken ×0.7; Sunder ×0.65; Armor Penetration ignores a fraction.
  • Damage types (7): physical, fire, frost, arcane, nature, shadow, holy. Every enemy kind carries a deliberate resist AND vulnerability profile (see the bestiary §9) — themes inherit them, so a warband reads strong against some damage types and soft to others, and the theme name carries tactical information.
  • Vulnerabilities: a unit can be soft to a damage type — a negative resist, typically 25% extra damage taken from it. Hits on a vulnerability flash WEAK! over the target and are tagged in the combat log; nature-soft units also take amplified Poison ticks (and fire-soft units amplified Burn ticks). Vulnerabilities are counterplay, never hidden: they show on the map's warband hover BEFORE committing to a fight, and on every unit's nameplate hover in battle.
  • Barrier absorbs damage (not BD) before HP.

4.4 Break damage & Break

Attacks deal Break damage (BD) — damage against the Break meter — equal to the ability's Break damage (BD) value × grade multiplier (×1.5 on crits). Every unit's Break meter is a universal 0–100 scale with a numeric readout on the bar. Logged BD is the applied (post-Constitution) value, so the number always matches the meter; crits deal ×1.5 BD, which is why the same ability moves different meters by different amounts. Resistance comes from Constitution: incoming BD is scaled by 100/Constitution (100 = neutral; a 160-Constitution boss takes ~38% less Break damage; an 85-Constitution mage takes ~18% more). Constitution is set per specialization role (see §6), raised by talents and Poise runes. When the meter fills, the unit BREAKS: it loses its next turn, armor ×0.7, +25% crit chance against it, and enemies actively exploit Broken heroes with their hardest affordable attack. Bosses cannot be Stunned unless Broken (stuns fizzle with an IMMUNE flag). Breaking triggers hitstop, a purple fracture flash, and a camera punch. After the lost turn, the Break meter resets to 0. Ward halves incoming BD; the Devout's Devoutness talent cuts it 20%; Bulwark of Fortitude negates it entirely.

4.5 Bleed & damage over time

  • Bleed is a 0–100 buildup meter (chip shows Bl##). Wounding attacks add buildup (Wildstrikes 35 at 50% per target; Hack and Slash 25 at 50% per hit). At 100 the target bleeds out for 20% max HP and the meter resets. Bleedout damage ignores armor entirely.
  • Burn: 6% of the applier's Attack (snapshotted when it lands; ≈6 at 100 Atk) at the start of the afflicted unit's turn; ticks can crit using the caster's snapshotted crit chance. Burn counts as fire damage — fire resists reduce the tick and fire vulnerability raises it, exactly as nature does for Poison. Reapplication extends duration — every fresh Burn ADDS its applied turns to the running timer (a 3-turn Fireball on a burning target = +3 turns, announced with a "Burn +N turns" float).
  • Poison: 3% of the applier's Attack per stack (snapshotted; ≈3 at 100 Atk) at the start of the afflicted unit's turn. Poison counts as nature damage — nature resists reduce the tick (and nature vulnerability raises it). Stacks additively with no cap; every new application adds a stack AND refreshes the timer. Applied by Orc Archers' Poison Arrow (3 turns), the Withered Warden's Roots of Wrath (3 turns), and the Survivalist's Trapper passive (5 turns).
  • Renewal (15% of the caster's max health, snapshotted at cast) and Focus (status) +10 Mana at turn start.

4.7 Items in battle

The party inventory is shared and opens as a picker via Alt or the "[Alt] Items" button (Tab cycles, Space uses, X closes — see §10). 4.8 Enemy AI

Enemies hunt the most wounded hero 40% of the time and pick randomly otherwise; if any hero is Broken they exploit it with their hardest affordable hit. Mocked enemies must attack the taunting Warrior — a taunt also overrides support casts. Support behaviors run before attacks: the Orc Shaman casts Healing Wave on the most wounded ally under 40% HP (friendly tanks and healers first); the Orc Shieldmaster ALWAYS casts Shielding when no ally carries its ward (lowest-HP ally first, itself included); the Withered Warden has a 60% chance to cast Wild Growth on the most wounded of its warband (itself included) when someone is below 70% HP. The Chief and the Ash-Wrought Tyrant build and spend Rage like a Warrior.

4.9 There is no announcer

All battle information lives in the scrolling combat log (top right) and floating combat text. No banner text appears at the top of the battle.

  • Rage (Warrior): starts at 0. +5 at the start of each of his turns; +10 whenever he is hit; every attack builds Rage (Strike 20, all spec/talent attacks 10); Perfect Strike +10.
  • Mana (Mage/Cleric/Hunter): +12 at the start of their turns; the Hunter's Perfect basic adds +10; potions/Rally restore more.

Spent by Coup de Grâce (which reads at most 200) and One Shot (which fires at 200).
  • Mercy (HOLY passive, 0–5): only the Holy spec has the bar. +1 stack whenever a party member falls below 50% health (any damage source; crossings count each time). Each stack held: +5% healing done. Stacks are SPENT on Hymn of Hope (1), Divine Plea (2), and Resurrection (1) — costs are paid before the cast resolves, so a spender heals with what remains — or on Empowerment: the ✦ Empower toggle (hotkey C) arms the next supporting cast for +1 extra stack and a bonus effect. Empowered casts forgo their perfect bonus. Talents move the economy and nothing else: the window that earns a stack (Guardian Angel, to 65%), the ceiling (Martyr's Vigor, to 8), the opening stacks (Zealous Light, 2), what a stack is worth (Heavenly Aura, 12% each), the refund chance (Sanctified), and the Empower surcharge itself (Ardor waives it above 3 held; Avatar of Mercy waives it always and GRANTS a stack instead). At maximum stacks a further crossing is wasted unless Grace is taken, which turns it into healing for the ally who earned it. See §7.
  • Runaway Resonance (ARCANIST passive, NO MAXIMUM): only the Arcanist spec has the bar, and it is the only resource in the game with no ceiling. Three clauses:
    • No ceiling. +1 per damaging cast, +2 on a crit (parry counters grant nothing), and nothing removes it — it persists to the end of the battle. Talents move the build RATE, never a cap: Harmonics (Arcane Explosion builds 2), Attunement (a crit builds 3), Resonant Core (+1 on the first cast of each of his turns), Critical Mass (4 on every third crit), Cascade (+1 per cast at 10 or more stacks), Backlash (+1 per hit received), On the Edge (4 for surviving below 35% health), and Overcharge (once per battle, gain half your current stacks — all of them on a perfect cast).
    • The curve compounds. At N stacks the damage bonus is 1.5% × N(N+1)/2 and the damage taken penalty is 0.75% × N(N+1)/2 — the same triangular curve at half the step. Both ends uncapped. Conduit raises the damage step to 2% and the Magi's Wrath capstone doubles it to 3%; nothing modifies the taken step. Singularity pays BUILD RATE instead — critical hits build 2 additional Resonance and every enemy killed builds 3 — because damage is quadratic in the stack count and only linear in the step, so a build-rate capstone and a step capstone come out comparable rather than one dwarfing the other. Crit building is additive: base 2, Attunement sets it to 3, Singularity adds 2 on top for 5.
    • +1% critical chance per stack, linear — one stable term that does not compound, so the tooltip has something a player can hold in their head.
    Compounding rather than linear is the whole design: a linear per-stack term is a slope, a compounding one is a curve, and the difference is what makes him nothing early and everything late. At five stacks he is weaker than a flat +15%/stack Arcanist was; by twelve he has roughly doubled. The numbers, because a compounding figure nobody tabulates is a figure nobody can plan against:

6a. Ability progression

Every hero opens a run with its core attack plus exactly 3 spec abilities, and earns 2 more over the run — one from zone 1's boss and one from zone 2's. The end boss awards none: the run is over and the pick would be dead value. There is no cap and no swap step, so a full run ends at core + 3 + 2 = 6 abilities.

Each award offers up to 3 abilities the hero does not already own, drawn from its SPEC pool only. Abilities are spec-locked: the class-pool draw is gone. Spec pools are 2–5 deep, so an offer shows what exists rather than padding — a hero deep into its pool may be offered two. The pick is resolved on that hero's map card, and the triple is rolled the moment the award drops — re-opening the card never rerolls it.

The mini-boss award is different in kind: a generic ability upgrade, chosen from three, attached to one ability the hero already owns. Upgrades stack on the same ability, but the same upgrade cannot be taken twice in a run. The placeholder pool of four, until the dozen are authored: Honed (+50% damage), Quickened (−2 cooldown, minimum 0), Effortless (costs no Rage / Mana / Focus — Mercy is unaffected, so a free Resurrection is not on the table), Swift (arrives 25% sooner, never below 1.0 initiative delay). Three awards a run, one per mini-boss.

An upgrade is only ever offered on an ability it can change — Honed on something that deals damage, Quickened on something with a cooldown, Effortless on something that costs. Swift fits everything, because everything has an initiative delay. An upgrade with no eligible ability is dropped from the offer rather than paired with a dud, so an offer can show fewer than three.

Upgrades apply at battle spawn, after talents. The order matters: several talents set an ability's field rather than add to it, so an upgrade applied first would be silently overwritten. Upgrades go last and always win. The hero sheet assembles the kit the same way, so the numbers on the sheet are the numbers in the fight.

Where you can see them. An ability carrying any upgrade wears a gold ◆ on its label — on its battle action button and on its hero-sheet chip — and hovering either ends with a line naming the upgrades ("Honed · Swift"). Names only: the magnitudes are already in the numbers above, which reflect the upgrade. Outside a fight, each map hero card carries a ◆N count beside its rune slots whose hover lists them one per line. A run awards three upgrades to every hero, twelve across the party, and before these three surfaces existed all twelve were discoverable only by hovering ability buttons mid-fight.

The SPEC pool holds what only that spec can earn: the abilities trimmed out of its opening three, the abilities its talent tree grants, and its own vaulted ones. An ability a talent has already granted is never offered again.

7. Talents

Structure: every tree is three LANES × seven ROWS, plus a capstone row — 24 nodes, and all twelve specs are purpose-designed on it.

A row holds one node per lane, and the player picks exactly ONE of the three. That pick locks the other two in that row permanently: they stay visible and greyed, with the reason in the tooltip — the player must see the door they closed. Before a pick is made, the tooltip also names the two doors it would shut.

Row 1 is open from the start; row N opens when row N−1 has been picked. There is no lane requirement anywhere — a build can zig-zag across all three lanes, or run straight down one.

The capstone row (row 8) opens when all seven rows are picked. Take one, from any lane; there is no lane-purity requirement. It is one capstone per hero, ever — the shelf says so, and taking one locks the other two with a tooltip naming what barred them.

Every node costs exactly 1 point and holds a single rank — no exceptions, no ranked investment, no escalating price curve. A complete tree is 8 nodes.

Economy: 1 point for choosing a spec (paid the moment the awakening is confirmed), then 1 apiece from every elite, mini-boss and boss — the end boss included. Regular fights award none. A zone holds 4 point-paying encounters (2 elites, a mini-boss, a boss), so a three-zone run pays 12 from the road plus the awakening's 1 = 13, against a complete tree of 8. The surplus buys row flexibility, and it does so by arithmetic rather than by a second wallet: rows are mutually exclusive and there are only eight of them, so a purse can open at most 8 rows and anything beyond that has nowhere to go but second nodes in rows already picked. (The Waystone Shard relic's +3 at run start and the point-granting events are earliness, not extra nodes.)

THE SURPLUS buys second nodes. With 13 points against 8 rows, five points cannot open anything — so they buy a second node in a row already picked, forcing that one row's door back open. The third node in that row stays shut forever. A separate "surplus" purse still exists in the data (old saves, and any future relic that wants to grant flexibility without granting climb); it is spent first when it holds anything, and ordinary points cover these picks when it is empty.

Layout: every tree renders as three lane columns — seven rows deep, with a capstone shelf underneath. Lane headers count the nodes taken in each lane (a read of the build, not a requirement). Each node is an emblem square labelled with its row number, and greys out the moment it is unreachable — a row not yet open, a door a sibling shut, or a capstone before 7/7. Art is placeholder.

A row is drawn as one decision, not three picks. Each of rows 1-7 sits inside a band labelled CHOOSE ONE; hovering any node dims its two siblings, so the choice is visible at the moment it is being made; and a row that has been decided shows its siblings greyed with a lock glyph. The capstone shelf carries the same treatment under its own stricter label.

Tooltip display: a node's tooltip shows its row, its one value, what it costs and from which purse, and — for a node not yet taken — the names of the two siblings taking it would close (capstones included). Never a bare disabled node with no reason given.

An earned ability no longer kills its own tree node. Several abilities sit in a spec pool and in that spec's tree, so a boss can hand one over before its node is reached. When that happens the node upgrades the ability instead of granting it: either an authored effect (the Arcanist's Overcharge node buys a second use per battle) or the generic fallback — the highest-priority ability upgrade the ability does not already carry, in the order Honed → Quickened → Effortless → Swift, filtered by the same eligibility rules the mini-boss pick uses. It does not consume the once-per-run allowance on those upgrades. If every eligible upgrade is already on the ability the node grants nothing, and its tooltip says so.

Talent visibility: every talent proc is written to the combat log with a "Talent:" tag (Enraged, Unrelenting Assault, Endurance, Unkillable, Richocet, Tank and Spank, Rallying Cry, Bulwark Line, Dominant Presence, Sundering, Riposte...), and stateful talent buffs display as status chips with live counters — Enraged / Iron Will / Crushing Blows / Battle Shout show their current % bonus, Endurance its armor streak, Shieldwall its Block bonus and Interpose its remaining charges, Bulwark Line the Block chance it lends each ally, Elemental Weakness the resist reduction, Unrelenting Assault its borrowed Constitution.

8. Economy

  • Gold: start 60 (+80 with Gravewrought Coin). Fight 45–60, elite and mini-boss 80–100, boss 110–130.
  • Talent points: spec choice 1, then elite 1 / mini-boss 1 / boss 1 and fight 0 — 4 a zone, 12 a run, 13 with the awakening, against a complete tree of 8 (§7).
  • Abilities: two picks per run, from zone 1's and zone 2's bosses, three offered each time and drawn from that hero's spec pool only. The end boss awards none. Each mini-boss instead awards a generic ability upgrade, three offered — three a run (see §6a).
  • Consumable prices: Health 30, Mana 30, Bomb 45, Revive 80, Defense 40. Starting inventory: 2 Health, 1 Mana, 1 Bomb, 1 Revive, 1 Defense.
  • Runes (run-scoped gear, bought at the Peddler or chosen from elite caches — the in-run power source that scales with depth): an authored pool (data/runes.json). Rarity means kind, not magnitude: Common = flat stat adds (the six generated "Cracked" templates remain the filler family: +10 max HP / +2% armor / +4 Speed / +12 Constitution / +2% crit / +8 max Mana; 50g), Rare ("Polished", 100g) alters an existing ability's numbers or grants a talent-like effect (talent-counter runes STACK with the talent), Epic ("Radiant", 160g) grants an ability, changes a damage type, or inverts a rule. Scarred runes — a flag on Rare/Epic with their own prefix and colour — buy a larger upside with a real cost, and are priced below their clean peers so the trade shows in the shop. Rarity odds deepen with the zone slot: 60/30/10 → 40/40/20 → 25/45/30 (Common/Rare/Epic). Eligibility: every rune declares a scope — universal, class-wide, or spec-only, so the awakening shapes the loot table — and ability-modifying runes only roll for heroes who actually own the named ability (boss trophies included). Three equip slots, flat, from run start — no growth ladder (swapped on the hero's map card). Shop offers avoid duplicates per hero, and an exhausted pool falls back to the Common family — the offer list never comes back empty.
    Heroes begin the run with no runes and three empty slots. The first rune comes from the Peddler, an elite cache, or a severity-3-or-4 bargain. Every pick-of-three is resolved on that hero's map card, and the three are always distinct: each candidate is drawn from a pool that already excludes the ones beside it, so a triple can never offer the same rune twice. A card whose hero owes a pick wears the rune-purple border until it is spent.
    Spec coverage: 65 authored runes — 5 universal, 3 class-wide for each of the four classes, and 4 spec runes for every one of the twelve specs. Each set is built to one rule: one rune per talent lane, plus one splash that carries a term from every lane and pays for reaching outside one. Exactly one rune per set is Scarred, and it sits on a lane; splashes are always clean. The per-lane rule is what makes a rune a build decision rather than a power increment — the same rune is worth more to a hero whose points went elsewhere than to one already deep in that lane.
  • Relics (permanent meta): one random unlock per boss kill, stored in user://relics.json; up to 3 equipped per run at the draft (scrolling shelf; events can slot a run-scoped extra via relic_grant). Pool: 25 relics — 17 common, 8 rare (◆) — every effect expressed through an audited hook vocabulary (19 hooks, listed atop relics.gd: run-start gold/points/items, party stat boosts at battle spawn — attack, typed damage, max HP, armor, speed, crit, Constitution, resistances, resource floor — victory heal/mana/gold, gold-find, shop discount, elite loot; the rest_heal_add hook is DEAD — rest nodes no longer exist, and the two relics carrying it, Cairnmoss Poultice and Martyr's Knucklebone, are left as written pending a design pass). Scalar hooks SUM and dict hooks merge across the equipped set, so stacking is well-defined. Adding a relic on an existing hook is pure data. Highlights: Waystone Shard grants +3 talent points; the elemental trio (Emberheart / Frostbound Sigil / Nightbloom Charm) covers all six non-physical damage schools; Prism Veil gives 10% resistance to every element; The King's Ledger compounds gold.
  • Persistent profile (user://profile.json, outside the run save): counts runs started/completed/wiped/forfeited per spec, bosses killed by kind, events seen (with repeats), and zones cleared. Forfeits are their own bucket, never folded into wipes — abandoning a run is not the same event as dying in one, and mixing them would make the wipe count unreadable the moment the exit gets used. Chronicle only — it gates nothing; the Relics screen shows the tally ("Cycles survived / lost / Events witnessed"). It is the substrate for a future persistent-unlocks design pass (sketch: persistent-unlocks-sketch.md in the workspace); sims never write to it.

11a. The Glossary

Why it exists: the tooltip culture explains numbers — a warband's resists, an ability's real damage, a talent's live value. Nothing explained a system. A player who does not know what Pressure does, or that Bleed never decays, or that relics outlive a run while runes do not, is playing a different game than the one that was built.

Shape: an authored pool in data/glossary.json read by scripts/glossary.gd — the same data pattern as Enemies / Events / Relics / Runes, so adding a term is a JSON edit and needs no code. Each entry carries an id, term, category, a one-line short (used for inline surfacing), the full long explanation, and optional see_also ids that render as working cross-links.

Coverage — seven categories: Combat flow (the initiative timeline and what delay does, the skill check, Pressure & Break, crit / miss / parry, the fallen returning at 20%), Statuses (every debuff the battle code can apply, plus Quick Draw), Damage types (all seven schools and how resists and vulnerabilities read), Resources (Rage and Mana, plus every spec resource: Resonance, Mercy, Empower, Faith, Focus, Loyalty, Ruin), Progression (the seven exclusive rows and the capstone row, the flat 1-point price, talent point income, boss trophies, ability upgrades, node scaling), Items & gear (runes, scarred runes, consumables, and relics versus runes as its own entry — permanent-across-runs versus run-scoped is the single most confusable distinction in the game), and The Run (zones and tiers, node types, elites, the bargain, modifiers and severity, events, what a wipe costs, the difficulty choice). The bargain trio was added when the modifier pool went from six to nineteen: a system with nineteen faces needs one place that explains the system rather than nineteen descriptions that each explain one face.

Surfacing: a panel from the map burger, the hero sheet, and the battle burger — a category tree on the left, the entry on the right with clickable cross-links. In battle it is inert: pure Control UI with no awaits or timers, and the battle scene suspends its own raw input handling while the panel is open so a click cannot grade a live skill check through the overlay. The timeline keeps running underneath, unchanged. Contextual entry point: a status chip's tooltip appends its glossary line when that adds information — the chip explains the effect, the glossary line names the system.

Authoring rule (load-bearing): entries are written from the CODE, at the read sites, never from this document. A glossary that teaches intent rather than behaviour is worse than none — it would make a tester confidently wrong instead of merely uncertain. Where an entry and this document disagreed, the code won and the document was corrected.

11b. First-Run Orientation & the Run Summary

Orientation — two cards, neither a tutorial mode, both dismissible and both shown once ever (gated on persistent Profile flags, set on dismissal, so a returning player is never re-taught):

  • The framing card, on the first map of a first run: what a run is — climb the tiers, the unavoidable mini-boss halfway up, the two ability picks a zone pays out, kill the zone boss, three zones, death ends the run, but a felled boss leaves a relic behind forever.
  • The skill-check pointer, at the first timing bar of the first battle: the sweeping marker, the gold and green windows, and what each grade is worth (Perfect +15% damage and +25% Break plus per-ability bonuses; Sloppy 60% damage and half Break). The design's premise is that execution matters as much as the build — a player who never notices the window is playing a materially worse game and would report it as shallow.

The run summary replaces both end panels, and serves all three endings — completion, wipe, and forfeit. It reports the outcome and depth reached ("Forfeited — Zone N …" reads distinctly from "Wiped", in its own colour, and carries the reason the player picked), the final battle (type, theme, and lineup) and which heroes fell, the party as it stood (per hero: spec, talent count with its lane spread, runes equipped, abilities earned), damage share per hero across the whole run, the run economy (battles won, elites taken, gold earned / spent / unspent, rests, events seen, relics carried), and a chronicle line — cycles survived, cycles lost, and cycles abandoned once there are any — so a session of runs accumulates into something visible. A Copy summary button puts a plain-text version on the clipboard: a wipe becomes a report the player can paste back to the designer, which is feedback data with no telemetry to build. If any debug tool was used at any point in the run, the summary opens with DEBUG TOOLS WERE USED IN THIS RUN — not a clean data point. — in the panel and in the clipboard text both (see §12, Testing shortcuts).

Two structural rules behind it: (1) the summary snapshots run state before the save is cleared, never by reordering the save logic — a run that stays resumable after death is a worse bug than a missing summary. (2) The per-run ledger (damage per hero, gold, elites, rests, battles) lives on the Run autoload, not the battle scene, because the battle scene reloads between fights; it is banked once per battle from a battle-local slice, written only in real play so the simulation harness's own statistics path can never double-count it.

12. Technical Reference

  • Scenes: main_menu, draft, spec_choice, map, battle, party, shop, settings, relics. All UI is built in code (no editor scenes).
  • Autoloads: Run (run state + saves), Settings, Music (looping stream player that survives scene changes; idempotent play()). Static data: Classes, Talents, Relics, Ability.
  • Saves: user://run_save.bin (auto after every node; versioned dictionary), user://relics.json, user://settings.cfg.
  • Balance tooling: DOD_AUTOPLAY=1 (one bot battle with debug prints), DOD_SIM=N (N max-speed battles + a stats report: win rate, damage shares, ability usage, roll rates), DOD_SIM_SPECS="berserker,cryomancer,inquisitor,beastmaster" (override the specs the bot plays — order: warrior, mage, cleric, hunter), DOD_SIM_ENEMIES="boss,shieldmaster,shaman" (force the enemy lineup in autoplay/sim/standalone battles), DOD_SIM_TALENTS="bz_bloodcraze:3,wd_toughness:2" (force-learn talents on bot heroes whose spec tree holds the id), DOD_SIM_ABILITIES="Resurrection,Divine Plea" (append pending talent-gated abilities to the bot hero whose spec owns them — Holy only for now), DOD_ENEMIES_OFF=1 (arm the "Enemy attacks OFF" debug toggle from the environment). Autoplay debug runs echo every combat-log line to stdout as "[LOG] ..." so headless tests can grep them; status expiries print as "... fades from ..." lines.
  • The contribution table (printed by the standalone report, the sweep and the run harness alike, so all three read the same way). One row per hero: dmg/b and dmg% (damage per battle, and its share of the party's), heal/b, prev/b (instrumented mitigation — blocks, barriers, stances, Faith; base armor and resists are deliberately not a contribution), BD/b and BD% (Break damage dealt, and its share of the party's), BDprev/b (Break damage refused or reduced), st/b (statuses landed on others), and d+h+p%.
    d+h+p% is exactly what it says: (damage + healing + damage prevented) over the party's total of the same three. It is NOT a share of the party's work. Break points and hit points are different units, and adding them needs an exchange rate nobody can defend — so Break stays out of that column entirely and gets the two columns of its own instead. A build that pays in Break (the Warden's Threat lane, the Occultist's grind, the Devout's Devoutness) reads low in d+h+p% by construction, and reading that as a weak build is the mistake the columns exist to prevent.
    Beneath the table, Break prevented per battle by source — Devoutness, Bulwark of Fortitude, Hold the Line, Ward, Immovable, Bracing — booked through one door, so the parts always sum to the BDprev/b column. Base Constitution is not booked (a stat block is not a contribution) and neither are the run modifiers Muffled and Deadened (nobody in the party did that).
  • Difficulty sweep: ./sim.sh --sweep N (DOD_SIM_SWEEP=1) runs N battles at EACH power budget 3/6/9/12 with a fresh warband rolled per battle from the fight-node themes, and prints — per budget — win rate, rounds, hero deaths, average enemy count, average enemies still alive entering round 3 (the field-size proxy AoE feeds on), and each hero's damage share. DOD_SIM_ZONE picks the roster, DOD_SIM_THEME narrows the roll to one theme — and because the share breakdown is per budget, a themed sweep reads out one theme's damage-share profile for free (the follow-up tool for any AoE-vs-numbers question). Sweep enemies are UNSCALED (no tier stat scaling) and sweep heroes carry base kits, so the sweep isolates the composition axis; a real tier-8+ fight is harder than its budget row. Without --sweep, plain sims fight a fixed raider/chief/archer/archer lineup (power 7, unscaled) — fine for kit smoke tests, meaningless for difficulty. Win-rate targets attach to a budget, never to "the sim": the ≈85% target describes the top of the ladder (see the changelog's Batch R sweep table for the measured curve).
    Run it at TWO build levels, because they are two different questions and each maps onto a real part of the run. Ungeared — no talents spent, the party as it enters zone 1: read the LOW budgets off this. All four force-learned into full 8-node lanes (DOD_SIM_TALENTS with four whole lanes) — the party at zone 3, where 8 points is exactly a full path: read the TOP budget off this. A single build level makes either the early game or the late game look wrong for a reason that is not real.
  • Run harness: ./sim.sh --run N (DOD_SIM_RUN=N, default 50) plays N COMPLETE runs headlessly — the real 36-encounter road, real warbands at real budgets WITH enemy tier scaling and the zone slot multiplier, HP/mana/items carried between battles, talent points earned and spent, elite runes and boss picks collected, all three zone slots — and prints the run report: completion/wipe rates, wipe distribution, per-slot win/death/HP averages, and the measured party-vs-warband power table (the progression counterweight, measured instead of modelled), plus the run economy: gold earned/spent/unspent, items used and carried unused, merchants met and events fired, and which bargains the bot accepted with their average severity — the one agency a run has left, and so the most diagnostic line in the report. The bot is dumb on purpose; every policy is printed in the report header. Its bargain policy is severity-extreme: it takes the harshest option offered while the party is above 60% average HP and the mildest once it is below, which exercises both ends of the table without pretending to model a payoff. DOD_SIM_ROUTE is retained but has nothing left to choose — a line has no route decision, and the report's "choice" figure reads 0% forever by design rather than being deleted. Shops are played by policy (heal-first: any hero under 50% HP buys a Health Potion; then the priciest affordable offer not already carried, runes included but only onto a member with a free slot, equipped at purchase; no buy dips below the 40g reserve — DOD_SIM_SHOPS=off restores the old buy-nothing floor). In battle the bot drinks a carried Health Potion when it opens a turn below 35% HP (DOD_SIM_ITEMS=off restores the never-drinks floor; sweep and standalone battles always stay dry so their baselines hold). DOD_SIM_BUILDS="spec:LaneName,..." names the talent lane each spec buys down (default: the tree's first lane, cheapest node first, capstone the moment its gate opens), DOD_SIM_TROPHIES lists preferred ability picks (default: the first offer entry), and DOD_SIM_RELICS arms relics at the draft. DOD_SIM_MAP, DOD_SIM_MINIBOSS, DOD_SIM_START_RUNE and DOD_SIM_SPEC_OPENING are retired — the branching map, the dealt mini-boss row and the opening rune pick they controlled no longer exist, so a Matrix row now reads map=line and carries no such fields. Rows from before the road are not comparable with rows after it: even depth changed units (36 encounters, not 33 tiers). Still excluded, stated in the output: bomb/revive/defense/mana items are never used in battle, no pre-emptive or offensive item use, routing sees one tier ahead only, and shop rune picks ignore build synergy. Simulated runs live entirely in memory (Run.sim_run): they never touch the player's save, relic unlocks, or profile.
  • Testing shortcuts: debug tools are ALWAYS available in dev (unexported) builds; exported builds require DOD_DEBUG=1 (and DOD_DEBUG=0 forces the gate shut in a dev build, which is how the headless tests stand where an exported build stands). The map burger menu gains a DEBUG section — +200 gold, +200 talent points for everyone, full heal, jump to the boss tier, advance to the next zone, Reroll Specs (refunds spent talent points, clears all specs, and reopens the awakening screen), and an All Spec Abilities Unlocked toggle (while checked, battles spawn with every talent-gated and trophy ability pre-granted — session-scoped, never saved, default OFF so normal play and sims measure real gated progression; headless runs arm it with DOD_SIM_GRANT_ALL=1).
  • Reaching any node on demand. Two tools sit in the same DEBUG section. Free Travel is a check item: while it is on, every node on the board is clickable — every tier, ignoring links and ignoring where the party stands, backwards as well as forwards. Clicking one advances the run honestly: the party really moves, the node is really marked visited, the tier readout reads the real tier, a fight is scaled by the tier it actually sits on, and the node books its tally and its Profile entry exactly as it would have. Nodes the toggle alone makes reachable are drawn as a fourth state, separate from visited / reachable / unreachable: a desaturated fill inside a magenta debug outline, with a [DEBUG] tooltip prefix, and a DEBUG: FREE TRAVEL marker sits in the corner while it is on.
    Test a node ▸ is a submenu — Shop, Rest, ??? Event, Fight, Elite — that runs one in place. A summoned node does not move the party, does not touch the tier, and does not mark anything visited; the board is exactly as it was when the tester returns. Everything else is real: a summoned shop has real offers and spends real gold, a summoned rest really heals, a summoned fight is a real battle at the current tier's scaling, with real rewards. (There is no Summon Boss: "Jump to Boss Tier" already goes there.)
  • Debug visits do not count. A summoned node is excluded from the two records that are read as data: it never bumps the run ledger (battles, gold, rests, elites, damage) and never books anything in the persistent Profile — including the wipe, if the party dies in a summoned fight. One session flag guards all of it. Free travel is the opposite case and is deliberately excluded from this rule: the party really is there and really did the thing.
  • The event picker. "??? Event" does not draw an event — it lists every event in data/events.json by title and id, each row stating whether its requirements currently pass or, if not, which condition failed (read from the same check that filters the real draw, so the two can never disagree). Requirement-failing events are selectable on purpose — testing the greyed-out branches is the point, and the event screen still runs its own guards. Already-seen events are repeatable, and picking one neither burns the run's draw pool nor tells the chronicle.
  • The honesty flag. Using any debug tool, on either menu, marks the run for the rest of its life (saved with it; unchecking a toggle never clears it). The end-of-run summary then opens with DEBUG TOOLS WERE USED IN THIS RUN — not a clean data point., in the panel and in the Copy summary clipboard text both, because the summary is the alpha's feedback channel and a debug-touched run is not a data point. Simulated runs can never set it: they load neither the map nor the battle menus.
  • In battle, a DEBUG ▾ menu (bottom-right) offers: Full Restore (HP/resources/secondary for the party), Kill All Enemies (every living enemy is switched off — no damage event, no on-hit or on-death proc — and the normal victory flow runs: gold, talent points, the heal, the bargain's reward, elite spoils and the summary), a Cooldowns OFF toggle (while checked, hero abilities never go on cooldown; existing cooldowns clear on enabling — enemies carry none), an Enemy attacks OFF toggle (enemies skip their turns while checked), and a turn lock — pick a hero and every HERO turn is theirs until you unlock (re-select them) or lock a different hero, while enemies keep attacking on their own turns (combine with Enemy attacks OFF to silence them); built for testing one hero's kit under fire. The displaced heroes' clocks advance as if they had acted, so the initiative timeline keeps flowing. The battle scene also runs standalone (godot res://scenes/battle.tscn) with a default party and no run state.
  • Sprites: horizontal frame strips named Name_Idle/Walk/Attack01–03/Hurt/Death.png (square frames; the stock sheets are 100×100, but units accept any frame size via the frame_size config — the Berserker sheet is 124×124, the Pyromancer 236×236). Partial sheets are fine: missing Attack02/03 replay Attack01, missing Hurt/Death hold the idle frame, and a single-frame Idle (the Pyromancer today) simply stands. Dedicated portraits are Name_Portrait.png (used on the nameplate, initiative bar, and party list). New assets arrive in "imported files/" (animated GIFs get converted to strips; 8-direction rotation GIFs — the east-facing frame is extracted).
  • Resolution template: all character art matches the Pyromancer — a standing figure ≈110–120px tall inside a 236px frame, rendered at 1:1 (never scaled), original colors. The Berserker (124px @1.25×) is legacy-sized and should be re-generated at the template when convenient. Battle backgrounds: authored at ≈1280×720 (up to ~1670×940) and shown at/near native size — never blown up.
  • Combat pacing: global PACE multiplier 0.85 on battle waits.
