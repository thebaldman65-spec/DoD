DAWN OF DECAY — THE FULL TEXT AUDIT

Every player-facing string, compared three ways: what the code does, what the code's description says, and what master.html says. This is a REPORT. Nothing here was fixed — no description was edited, no ability changed, no magnitude moved. The rewrite passes are scoped from this document and governed by text-standard.html.

THE HEADLINE, BEFORE THE BUCKETS

The numbers are sound. The prose is not. Every mechanically checkable relationship in the game came back clean, and that is the most important result here because it changes what the rewrite batches are: they are a prose job, not a repair job.

The finding that reframes the whole batch

Of the 120 draft ability rows in master.html, NOT ONE carries the code's sentence. All 120 are independently authored paraphrases, and the document's copy is a median 3.0x longer than the card's (118 of 120 are longer; the widest is 5.7x).

The two copies agree on the numbers and disagree on almost every word. The drift is not factual — it is that one of them was written to be read on a card and the other was written to explain a decision.

Blood Offering is the case to look at, because the batch brief quotes it as the calibration example for fluff — and the quoted text is the document's, not the card's:

CopyText
master.htmlHe loses 20% of his CURRENT health and gains 40 Rage (Perfect: 60). Because it is a share of what is left rather than of his maximum, it can never take him below 1 — and it costs less the lower he is.
classes.gd (what the player reads)Open a vein on purpose: lose 20% of your CURRENT health and gain 40 Rage. It can never take you below 1 — and the lower you are, the less it costs.
the code (battle.gd:14240)20% of current HP, floored at 1; 40 Rage, 60 on Perfect. Both copies are true.

The consequence for the rewrite batches: the fluff problem is smaller on the cards than the brief assumes, and larger in the document. The card is already two sentences instead of three and does not lecture. It still breaks the person rule and still carries a rationale clause — but a rewrite pass calibrated against the document's verbosity would over-correct text that is already most of the way there.

BUCKET 1 — WRONG

A number or condition that does not match the code. These are design questions, not typos. A tooltip that misstates what a card does means decisions have been made from bad information, and what needs deciding is whether the code or the intent was right.

1.1 — "Iron Will" is the name of TWO different mechanics, and the document documents only one of them

Severity: highest in the batch. Two unrelated things wear the chip name Iron Will:

In master.html §4.6ABSENTPresent — and it is the row that carries the name "Iron Will"

Why this is bucket 1 and not a naming quibble: the document's status table has one row called "Iron Will" and it describes the talent. A reader checking what Iron Will does gets the talent's answer for the ability's question. The two do not resemble each other — one is an immunity block with a flat cut, the other is a scaling mitigation with no immunities.

The game already has a deliberate same-label pattern, and this is NOT it. berserk/berserk_risk both read "Berserk" and formless/formless_recoil both read "Formless" — each pair is the upside and the price of ONE ability, distinguished by chip tag (Bk vs Bk!) and colour, and both halves are correct. Iron Will is two separate things that collided.

The decision owed: rename one of them, or accept the collision and give the document both rows. A rename is an ability/talent change and is out of scope for a text batch — which is exactly why this is reported rather than fixed.

1.2 — Battle Shout's registry tooltip hardcodes the lowest of three tiers

STATUS_INFO["battle_shout"] (battle.gd:72) reads:

+8% damage, plus 1% per 20 blood buildup on the enemy party (at cast time).

The code (battle.gd:16588) picks the base from battle_shout_node:

battle_shout_nodeHow it was obtainedBaseTurns
0earned from a pick, node never taken8%2
1the node granted it12%3
2the node upgraded an earned copy18%4

The registry string is right for one of the three cases and wrong for the other two, and it states no duration at all while the duration also moves (2/3/4).

Mitigating, and it should be weighed: the cast path calls update_status immediately after _apply_status and overwrites the chip with a live string that names the real total, the real base, the real turns and the real blood buildup. So the wrong text is a fallback that the live path replaces in the same loop — it is not believed to be reachable in normal play. It is reported because it is the only copy of Battle Shout's number that does not move with the node, and because a later batch that changes the tiers will not think to change it. master.html is CORRECT here — its row lists all three tiers.

BUCKET 2 — INCOMPLETE

Accurate as far as it goes, but omits something the player needs.

2.1 — THE STRUCTURAL ONE: the draft card shows the description and nothing else

This is the largest single finding in the audit and it is one code path. map_screen.gd:1202 renders ab.description on the draft card and nothing else — no computed damage, no Break damage, no cost, no cooldown, no initiative, and no Perfect. Every other surface that shows a description adds the computed lines beneath it.

The consequences compound:

  • 30 of the 66 damaging abilities never state their damage in the description, because the tooltip computes it. On the draft card those 30 cards show no damage figure at all. Ice Lance is the sharpest case: damage: 35 in the dict, and the card says only "+5% of Attack per Chilled stack on the target". On the draft screen it reads as a 5%-per-stack ability, understating it by 35% of Attack.
  • No card shows its Perfect bonus while being drafted. All 190 perfect_text strings are invisible on the one screen whose whole purpose is comparing cards.
  • Aimed Shot's card is, in full: "A perfect line. Patient, precise, final." Its dict is damage: 45, cost: 20, cooldown: 1, perfect_text: "+20 Focus" — the hardest-hitting card in the Sharpshooter's pool, and its description states none of it. On the draft card that is a blank card. It also spends its one sentence on the word "perfect", which is a reserved mechanic.

This is a code gap, not a text gap, and it cannot be closed by rewriting descriptions — writing the numbers into the prose would put arithmetic back on the mid-combat tooltip, which the standard forbids. Owed to a later batch: the draft card should render the same computed block the blacksmith card already builds.

2.2 — 53 of 61 timed abilities never say what a second cast does

61 ability descriptions grant an effect "for N turns". Eight say whether recasting refreshes, extends, stacks or replaces. Fifty-three are silent. The brief's claim that this is "absent almost everywhere" is confirmed at 87%.

2.3 — The status chips are consistently thinner than the document

Of 78 status rows matched between STATUS_INFO and master.html §4.6:

  • 41 rows: the document states a number the chip omits — nearly always the duration (Decay "3 turns", Dazed "3 turns", Frostbite "2 turns", Bewitched "3 turns", High Guard "2 turns", Rallied, Stabilized...) or the source that applies it.
  • 0 rows: the chip states a number the document omits.
  • 2 rows differ in both directions: Ruin (chip omits the 40% lifesteal cap; document omits the every-10th-stack detonation) and Iron Will (bucket 1.1).

The asymmetry is the finding. The document is uniformly the more complete copy, so the chips are where the gaps are — and the chip is the only one a player reads mid-fight. Several chips are also vaguer than they need to be where the value is live and known: Shieldwall's chip says "bonus Block chance" where the document says "+25%", Elemental Weakness says "resistances reduced" where the document says "by 20%".

BUCKET 3 — PROSE ONLY

Correct and complete, just badly written or padded with rationale.

3.1 — Person: split in both directions, 130 of 204

86 ability descriptions use second person ("you", "your"). 44 use third person ("he", "his", "her"). Some use both. master.html is uniformly third person, so the document and the card disagree on voice for most of the game. The standard's answer is neither: no pronouns.

3.2 — 97 of 204 carry a bare "N turns"

Nothing in the sentence distinguishes a duration from a cooldown. The tooltip prints its own Cooldown: N turns line underneath, so a description restating the cooldown is a second copy that can drift.

3.3 — perfect_text is one field in four shapes

190 strings: a bare value ("+10 bonus Mana"), an "instead" comparison (107 of them — "60 Rage instead of 40"), a full sentence ("The debt pays 35% of maximum health instead of 25%"), and compounds ("+20% parry instead of +15%, and holds 6 turns instead of 4"). The rendered label is uniform because every surface supplies "Perfect: " itself — the inconsistency is entirely inside the field.

Four abilities also state the Perfect condition inside the description — Deadfall, Pommel Strike, Aimed Shot, Snare Trap — so the tooltip says it twice.

3.4 — passive_desc: the known offender, now measured

The twelve blocks are the worst text in the game against the standard, and the measurement is exact. They render in an autowrapping 400px label at font size 13 (party_screen.gd:253), whose budget is 61 characters a line. 23 of their 67 authored lines are wider than that, running to 71 characters, so the hard breaks and the soft wrap fight and the block comes out ragged. Five specs are affected: Arcanist, Holy, Inquisitor, Occultist, Beastmaster.

The Arcanist's is the single worst string in the game and it is the exhibit for the no-arithmetic rule — correct to the decimal, and unreadable:

Runaway Resonance: damaging casts build stacks (2 on a crit) with NO MAXIMUM, and nothing removes them. The bonuses COMPOUND: at N stacks +1.5% x N(N+1)/2 damage and +0.75% x N(N+1)/2 damage taken, plus a flat +1% crit per stack. 5 stacks = +22%/+11%; 12 = +117%/+59%.

It puts a triangular-number formula in algebra on the hero sheet, then works two examples. The stack count is known at display time, so the live value is computable and the formula does not need to be there at all.

One code inconsistency found alongside it: spec_choice_screen.gd:126 flattens \n to a space for ability descriptions and does not do the same for passive_desc on the very next line, so the passive block hard-wraps inside a 298px column on that screen.

3.5 — Rationale on the card

The dominant fluff pattern, and it is heavier in master.html than in the code (§0). On the cards it shows up as a trailing clause explaining why the number is what it is — Blood Offering's "and the lower you are, the less it costs", Reckless Abandon's "and three turns with nothing left to cast". Both are true, neither is a rule, and both belong in the changelog.

A second, subtler form: flavor that carries the mechanics. "Open a vein on purpose:" is the sentence's subject — remove the flavor and the sentence loses its verb. Flavor has to be removable, and across the corpus most of the opening clauses are not.

THE LENGTH CEILING — MEASURED, NOT GUESSED

Measured against the real theme font (Open Sans SemiBold) at the real sizes the screens set, over all 1,846 authored lines. The narrowest surface that shows a description is the draft card, 258px at font size 11.

  • Every observed line of 44 characters or fewer fits (widest measured 241px against a 258px budget).
  • 45 is the first length that overflows (263px).
  • The ability corpus already holds it: 936 of 936 lines. The existing hand-wrapping discipline is exactly right and needs no change.

Everything that overflows is either a field with no authored breaks at all (talent and rune text, which autowrap correctly and are fine) or passive_desc (§3.4).

COVERAGE — WHAT WAS AND WAS NOT VERIFIED

Stated plainly, because an audit that overstates its own reach is worse than a narrow one.

Mechanically checked, whole population: all 204 ability descriptions against their own dicts (cooldown, cost, damage, heal, applied-status duration); all 120 document stat cells; all 65 runes against their payloads; all 1,846 lines against the measured ceiling; the full style sweep (person, bare turns, Perfect notation, keyword capitalisation, literal \n); all 78 matched status rows document-vs-code.

Hand-read against battle.gd: the status constants named in the headline table; Blood Offering, Blood Price, Battle Shout, Iron Will, Ice Lance, the Resonance curve, the Ruin lifesteal cap and its Gluttony/Soul Leech terms, the generic upgrade fallback behind the Rune of the Last Rites.

NOT exhaustively verified: the 324 talent nodes against their runtime behaviour. Talent payloads are mostly flag values (ranks: 1) whose magnitudes live in battle.gd and unit.gd, so 92 nodes state a number their own payload cannot produce — this is expected and is not itself a finding, but it means each of the 92 needs a read of its own read-site to confirm. Also not exhaustively verified: the 93 glossary entries against behaviour, and the enemy and event text. Those three are the honest gap in this audit and are the obvious scope for a follow-up.
