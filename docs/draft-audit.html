DAWN OF DECAY — THE DRAFT AUDIT

Every one of the 142 draft cards, compared against every other card it can be offered beside, against the protected core kit of the hero who would be offered it, and against the talent tree that hero allocates into. This is a REPORT. Nothing here was fixed — no card was retired, no magnitude moved, no description edited. Every finding is a design question, and each is written so the remedy can be ruled on directly.

COVERAGE, STATED BEFORE ANY FINDING

An audit that overstates its reach is worse than a narrow one that admits its edges. Batch CJ reported 120 of 120 clean and was later found to have had no check at all for the failure mode it missed. So: this is what was compared, how, and what was not.

WHAT WAS NOT CHECKED, AND SHOULD NOT BE READ AS CLEAN.

  • Nothing here is a balance judgement and no simulation was run. "Too strong" appears only where it means strictly dominates another card in the same pool, which is a correctness matter. Whether any card is well-tuned is the designer's, and no sim has run since Batch DK — every carried figure in docs/state.md is already stale.
  • The two Grade-1/2 findings were confirmed by reading code. The Grade-3 and Grade-4 findings were not measured — they are structural claims about what a card decides, argued from the payload. A sim could refute one.
  • The axis vocabulary is this audit's own and is defensible rather than canonical. It was derived from the corpus; a different cut would move some cards by one column. The clusters are robust to that; the exact per-pool axis counts are not.
  • Enemy abilities, relics, runes and items were not compared against draft cards at all. The rune half in particular is a live question: docs/state.md already records that two runes grant a card their own hero can now draft.
  • Boss-pick pools (SPEC_POOLS / CLASS_POOLS) were dumped but not audited. They are a separate offer with a separate population and they were out of scope.

§1 — GRADE 1 AND GRADE 2: THE TWO CORRECTNESS FINDINGS

Grade 1 is the same card twice. Grade 2 is one card strictly better than another in the same pool, which the standing rule in classes.gd already forbids — "NO ABILITY MAY BE A STRICTLY BETTER VERSION OF ANOTHER IN THE SAME POOL". Both findings below are Grade 2, so both are a rule that has not been enforced rather than a matter of taste.

FINDING 1 — GLACIAL PRISON AND FLASH FREEZE ARE ONE CARD AT TWO PRICES, AND THE DEARER ONE IS ALSO THE SLOWER, THE RARER AND THE ONLY ONE THAT CAN BE WASTED

Both are in the Cryomancer's pool. Their handlers are the same three steps in the same order. Comments and cosmetics stripped, this is the whole of both:

"flash_freeze":                                "glacial_prison":

Nothing else. Flash Freeze computes one extra local, ff_boss, and uses it only inside its log string. The boss rule both cards advertise ("a boss resists until Broken", "the ice holds it one turn only") lives inside _hold_freeze and belongs to neither — Flash Freeze's card sells a shared property as though it were its own, which is why the two read as different cards and are not.

THIS WAS CREATED BY BATCH DO AND BY NOTHING ELSE. Glacial Prison was a talent grant, in no pool, until DO moved it into the pool that had held Flash Freeze since Batch CB. Neither batch did anything wrong on its own terms; the collision only exists once both are draftable.

AND THE SOURCE ALREADY SAYS ALL OF THIS — IN A COMMENT WHOSE BOTH REASONS HAVE SINCE BEEN RETIRED

This finding was not discovered. It was recorded, deliberately suspended, and then left standing while the two things holding it up were removed by other batches. The comment above Flash Freeze in classes.gd reads, in full:

# REPORTED, NOT RE-TUNED: on every number this is a strictly worse
# GLACIAL PRISON (Deep Freeze row 4 — 25 Mana, 2.5, 4cd for the same
# outright freeze). The distinction is the ACQUISITION CHANNEL, which is
# real: the node is a bought cell in ONE lane of ONE tree, so a Winter or
# Thaw Cryomancer can never have it, while this is drafted by any of
# them. Its PERFECT is what keeps it from being dominated outright — see
# `_hold_freeze`'s `force` argument, which no node buys.
the defencewhat happened to it
1. The acquisition channel. "the node is a bought cell in ONE lane of ONE tree, so a Winter or Thaw Cryomancer can never have it." DIED AT DO. Glacial Prison is a draft card in the Cryomancer's own pool now, available to every Cryomancer whatever lane he allocates. Deep Freeze row 4 is cr_numbing "Numbing Cold" (Ice Lance costs less, cooldown falls to 1) — the cell the comment points at grants nothing any more, because no node grants anything any more.
2. The Perfect. "Its PERFECT is what keeps it from being dominated outright — see _hold_freeze's force argument." DIED AT CR, AND A SUITE PINS IT SHUT. test_batch_ax asserts the absence of _hold_freeze(target, attacker, true) with the message "Flash Freeze no longer FORCES the freeze — a boss resists until Broken", and all four _hold_freeze call sites in battle.gd pass two arguments. And the card carries perfect_id "" and perfect_text "", with no is_perfect branch anywhere in its handler — it has no Perfect at all.

So the standing rule was consciously suspended for two stated reasons, and both reasons were later removed by batches that had no cause to look at this card. That is a more useful finding than "two cards are the same": the mechanism that failed is a justification outliving its premises — the same species as the stale ~96 denominator CD swept and the 53 of 204 figure DJ pinned. Correcting the comment is one edit and is owed whatever the designer rules on the cards.

THE CHOICE: retire one (Flash Freeze is the one with nothing of its own); differentiate (give Flash Freeze the clause its card already implies — a Broken boss held for a turn is a real effect no other card buys, and it is currently only a log line); or move Glacial Prison out of the spec pool. Doing nothing ships a card that is never the right pick.

RESOLVED AT BATCH DR §2 — FLASH FREEZE IS RETIRED. The choice taken was retire one, and Flash Freeze was the one with nothing of its own. The suspension comment was DELETED rather than updated, because both of its stated reasons were already dead — the finding this audit called "a justification outliving its premises" is now a standing trap in CLAUDE.md. The Cryomancer's pool goes 12 → 11 and the draft 142 → 141 before §4 takes it to 143. One further stale count of the same species was found while removing it: _hold_freeze's header said THREE callers when DO's Cryoclasm had made it four, and named a force no caller has passed since CR. See docs/reports/DR.md §2.

FINDING 2 — BATTLE POISE'S ENTIRE PAYLOAD IS A SUBSET OF ANSWERING STEEL'S, AND THEY SIT IN THE SAME POOL

Both are Swordmaster draft cards. Both are pure self-flags, so the payload is not in the handler — it is at the parry site in battle.gd, where both fire on the same event, eleven lines apart:

if strike_target.has_status("answering_steel"):
    ... resource += ANSWERING_RAGE          # 15 Rage
    _tick_cooldowns(strike_target, ANSWERING_TICK, "Answering Steel")      # ANSWERING_TICK == 1
if strike_target.has_status("battle_poise"):
    _tick_cooldowns(strike_target, BATTLE_POISE_TICK, "Battle Poise")      # BATTLE_POISE_TICK == 1
Ragecooldownturnsstance requiredparry chanceper parry
Answering Steel2046none+20%1 turn off every cooldown, and 15 Rage
Battle Poise2544Defensive—1 turn off every cooldown

Answering Steel is cheaper, lasts half again as long, has no stance requirement, pays the same cooldown tick from the same constant, and adds two clauses Battle Poise has not got — one of which (+20% parry) makes its own payoff fire more often. There is no board state in which Battle Poise is the better pick.

The source anticipates the pairing and answers a different question. The comment at the parry site says the two are "DELIBERATE STACKING PARTNERS rather than rivals — a single parry held under both takes TWO turns off everything he holds." That is a good reason to hold both once you own both. It is not a reason for the pool to offer both: in a three-card offer you take one, and Answering Steel is never the worse of the two. The stacking argument only starts paying after the domination has already decided the pick.

This one predates DO and neither card is one of the twenty-two — nothing moved them together, and it stood through every draft batch since. The source shows why it was missed, and the reason generalises. Answering Steel's own comment names Battle Poise explicitly: "BATTLE POISE is the deliberate stacking partner rather than a rival: both pay per parry, so a single turned blade takes TWO turns off every cooldown he holds." The author asked whether the two stack and never asked which of the two you take. Those are different questions, and a three-card offer only ever asks the second one — the stacking question does not arise until after a pick Answering Steel always wins.

THE CHOICE: differentiate — Battle Poise's stance requirement is currently a pure cost with nothing bought by it, so the obvious repair is to make the Defensive guard buy something Answering Steel cannot have (a second tick, or a parry that also refunds the ability); or retire Battle Poise; or move one of the two so the pool never offers the pair.

RESOLVED AT BATCH DR §3 — THE DEFENSIVE GUARD NOW BUYS SOMETHING. The choice taken was differentiate, on this section's own diagnosis that the stance requirement was pure cost with nothing bought by it. Once a turn, a parry under Battle Poise lets him GUARD CHANGE without spending an action — a clause Answering Steel cannot have, because it has no requirement to reward. It is the pivot alone (Guard Change's 15 BD, Sunder Guard's 40 to every enemy, No Quarter and Tempo stay on that card), it is bounded to once a turn, the cooldown tick still pays on every parry, and it asks _ability_usable about the REAL Guard Change — so Formless refuses it and its own 1-turn cooldown is respected, at one door rather than two. Answering Steel is untouched. See docs/reports/DR.md §3.

AND ONE GRADE-1 PAIR THAT NEVER COMPETES, REPORTED BECAUSE IT IS STILL THE SAME CARD TWICE

Aimed Volley (Hunter class-wide) and Magic Missiles (Mage class-wide) are the same card. Both are pure field vectors — zero bespoke code, no special — and both are "three strikes at one enemy for 12% of Attack each": Aimed Volley 20 Mana / cd 3 / init 2.5 / 8 Break a shot, Magic Missiles 15 Mana / cd 2 / init 2.0 / 3 Break a shot. They are in different class pools, so no hero is ever offered both, and the same is true of Hoarfrost Armor (Cryomancer: −25% damage taken, attackers gain Chilled) and Immolate (Pyromancer, one of DO's: −20% damage taken, attackers are set Burning), which are one card wearing two elements. Neither is a live collision. They are reported because a designer counting how many different things the game contains should know that the 142 hold fewer than 142 ideas.

§2 — PER-POOL AXIS COVERAGE, WHICH IS THE REAL ANSWER

The question is not whether two cards are duplicates. It is whether a pool holds more than one build. Two measures are given below because they disagree, and the disagreement is itself the finding.

  • DISTINCT DECISIONS — how many different (effect axis × target shape) pairs a pool covers. This is the answer to "how many cards would a player have to draft before the pool stops offering them anything new?": once a player holds one card per decision, the rest of the pool is variations on what they already own.
  • ENGINE-BOUND — how many of the pool's cards read or write the spec's own system. A pool can offer ten different decisions and still hold one build, if all ten are made with the same fuel.

THE TWO EXTREMES, LOUDLY

THE MOST CONCENTRATED POOL IN THE GAME IS THE SWORDMASTER'S: TEN CARDS, FOUR DECISIONS. Every card he can draft is one of four things — strike one enemy (Precision Strike, Feint, Sever, Lunge), change or hold a stance (Feigned Guard, Discipline, Formless), buy tempo off a parry (Battle Poise, Answering Steel — and one of those two is Finding 2), or execute (Execute). A Swordmaster who has drafted four cards has seen everything his pool decides. The eleventh through the sixteenth cards he could be offered are the Warrior class pool, which is where his healing, his area damage, his control and his party buff all live — his own spec pool contains none of those four.

THE MOST CONCENTRATED BY ENGINE IS THE BEASTMASTER'S, AND IT IS ABSOLUTE: 8 OF 8. Every card in his pool requires a living companion or reads the Loyalty a companion earned, and none of the eight is a heal, a shield, or damage mitigation for the hunter himself. Bloodbond is the closest and it points the other way: it refuses the blow that would fell the companion and moves a quarter of it onto the hunter, where it can kill him. His entire defensive option set for a whole run is two of the six Hunter class cards (Field Dressing, Camouflage). He also received none of DO's twenty-two, so nothing has been added to his pool since Batch CI.

THE CRYOMANCER'S 12 ARE 12 OF 12 ON THE ICE — six read or write the Glacial Hold, six spread or exploit Chilled, and there is not one card in the pool that works on a board with no Chilled on it. Four of the twelve do one thing: propagate Chilled from somewhere to somewhere else (Rimebinding, Deep Winter, Rime, Killing Frost), and two more freeze outright and are the same card (Finding 1). DO added four cards to this pool and none of them opened a new decision: Rime spreads Chilled, Glacial Prison freezes, Cryoclasm relocates a freeze, Shatter cashes the holds — the pool already did all four.

THE PYROMANCER IS THE CASE WHERE THE TWO MEASURES DISAGREE, AND BOTH ARE TRUE. His thirteen cards cover ten distinct decisions, the widest in the game — and twelve of the thirteen read or write Burn, the narrowest binding in the game. Overburn's multiplier is literally _total_burn_turns(), the sum of Burn turns standing on living enemies, so five cards in the pool are the same lever on the passive's own input: Ember Debt (lay 12 turns), Backdraft (+2 turns on everything alight), Stoke (double one target's), Slow Burn (stop the clock), Emberkeep (double what arrives). He has ten things to decide and one currency to decide them in. Whether that is a rich pool or a single build depends on a judgement this audit cannot make for the designer — but the numbers should not be read as ten builds.

THE LEAST CONCENTRATED IS THE WARDEN'S: NINE CARDS, NINE DECISIONS — NO TWO CARDS IN HIS POOL MAKE THE SAME ONE. That is the only pool in the game of which that is true, and it is worth saying plainly because the rest of this document is a list of problems. But the second measure catches what the first misses: four of his nine hang off one trigger. Aegis Wall, Anvil, Recompense and Turn the Blade all pay out on a block happening — they pay four genuinely different things (party healing, a Heavy Plating rule change, Rage, Break onto the attacker), which is why they score as four decisions, but a Warden who is not blocking holds four dead cards. Anvil and Recompense additionally cancel each other by design and both cards say so.

AND THE HUNTER CLASS IS THE STRUCTURAL FINDING UNDERNEATH ALL OF IT. Its three specs hold the three shallowest pools (8 each), received none of DO's twenty-two, and not one of the twenty-four Hunter spec cards is a heal, a shield, or damage mitigation for the hero. That is not a coincidence of three separate authoring passes: it lines up with docs/state.md's standing note that the Sharpshooter has no defensive node in his 27. For a Sharpshooter, both halves of his progression — the tree and the draft — offer him nothing defensive at all, and the only two cards in the game that can change that are Field Dressing and Camouflage, which he must be lucky enough to be offered from a pool that is one card in four.

WHAT THE AXES ACTUALLY ARE, AND HOW THE BRIEF'S LIST COMPARED

The brief expected "something like: single-target damage, area damage, sustained damage over time, burst, mitigation, healing, shielding, control, tempo and initiative, resource generation, status application, meter manipulation". Eleven of those twelve are real and one is not. "Sustained damage over time" is not a draft axis: exactly one card in 142 has a damage-over- time field as its primary payload (Penance), because in this game the DoTs are the engines — Burn, Bleed, Poison and Ruin are what the passives read — so a card that touches one is doing meter manipulation, not damage over time. It appears as a secondary tag on 13 cards and as a primary axis on one.

Five axes the brief did not name carry 16 cards between them and every one of them is load-bearing: Break/Pressure (3), damage amplification (12, split self and team — the third-largest axis in the game and absent from the brief's list entirely), death denial (3 — the Holy Cleric's largest cluster), cleanse (2), and the companion (3 primary, 5 more secondary). Mitigation had to be split three ways to say anything useful: self, one ally, and the whole hero side are three different cards, and the Devout's pool holds all three.

THE FINDING THE SCALE TABLE MAKES: A COOLDOWN OF ZERO MEANS SOMETHING IN THIS GAME, AND TWO OF THE TWENTY-TWO HAVE ONE

Every one of the twelve cooldown-zero abilities in the protected core kits is the hero's free basic attack — Strike, Smite, Fireball, Frostbolt, Quick Shot, Shadowrend, Arcane Explosion. Nothing else in the game is repeatable every turn. Of the 142 draft cards, exactly two have no cooldown, and both are DO's: Pyroblast and Lunge.

That is not an oversight in DO. It is the shape of a talent grant arriving intact: at the end of a lane the price was the node — nine rows of points — so no cooldown was needed to bound it. In a pool where every other card pays a cooldown, a repeatable card is a different kind of object, and the player is choosing between two objects that are not the same kind.

THE CHOICE: price them as draft cards (give both a cooldown, which is a retune and therefore the designer's), or rule that a repeatable card is a legitimate draft shape and say so in CLAUDE.md so the next author knows it is allowed. What should not happen is that it stays undecided, because the next batch that adds a card will copy whichever precedent it happens to read.

AND NINE OF THE TWENTY-TWO SHIPPED WITH A CLAUSE THEY CAN NO LONGER HAVE — THE COUNT IS TEN

DO recorded this honestly in classes.gd: an upgrade arm fires only where a node's GRANT collides with an earned copy, no node grants any more, so the nine fields those arms wrote are read-only-zero: battle_shout_node, lunge_upgraded, execute_upgraded, hold_line_upgraded, rampage_upgraded, overcharge_extra, intercession_long, resolve_extra_turns and bulwark_extra_turns. The fields and their read sites were deliberately left standing. That is a sound decision and it is not the finding.

The finding is that there is a tenth, and it is not in DO's list. icy_resolve_ranks is read twice — Rime's duration is 4 + attacker.icy_resolve_ranks, and the recast-refusal preview quotes the same expression — and nothing in the project writes it. Not talents.gd, not runes.gd, not relics.gd, not events.gd. The cr_icy_resolve node still exists and still carries that id, but DO re-pointed it onto Blizzard (protected core) for exactly the right reason, written in its own comment: "became a bet on a card the hero may never be dealt." The re-point was correct; the field it used to write was left behind.

Nothing is currently wrong at runtime — zero is the base, so Rime lasts its authored 4 turns. It is reported because it is a dead read of exactly the species DO enumerated as nine, no gate catches it (test_batch_cd's dead-symbol sweep does not reach it, the same way it does not reach FIREDRAW_TAKE), and the number "nine" will be quoted by the next batch that opens this thread.

§6 — THE THREE PLACES A COLLISION HIDES

1. THE CLASS POOL AGAINST THE SPEC POOL

The class pools are the healthiest thing in the draft and the audit should say so. The authoring rule — a class-wide ability is deliberately untied and general; it feeds no passive — is honoured at 0 of 24: not one class card reads any spec's engine. Three of the four cover six distinct decisions with six cards, which no spec pool does.

Two live cross-pool collisions were found, both in §3: Battle Shout ↔ Warcry (Berserker) and Drumfire ↔ Aimed Volley (Sharpshooter). And one structural one: the Cleric class pool is 3 of 6 healing (Ministration, Consecration, Undying Vigil), and it lands on top of two Cleric spec pools that already heal — giving the Devout six healing cards in a sixteen-card offer space, the largest single-axis cluster anywhere in the game. The Occultist is the one Cleric spec that wants none of them, and for him the class pool is a quarter of every offer.
