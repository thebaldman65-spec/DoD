## §2 — DOWNWIND CARRIES AFFLICTIONS, NOT A CARD'S OWN BOOKKEEPING (ruled)

### §2a — THE RULE, AND WHERE IT LIVES

> **Downwind copies an affliction. It does not copy a status that is a card's own bookkeeping — a binding, a slot, a counter
> the card reads. Copying bookkeeping makes a second instance of a thing the card counts, which is never what the card meant.**

Recorded where HU's carrier rule is (`CLAUDE.md`, DR §1's block, a sub-bullet of its own) and in `design-notes.md`. **In the
code it is a flag the card holds, not a list Downwind keeps**: `battle._carry_withheld`, set and cleared around the one
`_apply_status` call a card makes to lay its own bookkeeping, and read in Downwind's condition. **Why at the card**: the
bookkeeping is the card's — Snare Trap's slot is counted in `trap_count`, Vendetta's premise is in its handler — and a
taunt is bookkeeping when Vendetta lays it and an affliction when Mocking Blow does, so a list of ids at Downwind could not
tell the two apart. `check_hv` §2b drives the difference both ways.

### §2b — THE TWO DOWNWIND NO LONGER CARRIES (built)

- **Vendetta's lock.** What Downwind carried was the TAUNT Vendetta lays — `mocked` at −1 turns with the Warden's seat as
  its power — never the `vendetta` mark itself, which is a mark (`DISPEL_NEVER`), not an affliction, and was never copied.
  The copy locked a second enemy onto the Warden for the fight with no damage cut. **Driven** (`check_hv` §2b, with Carrion
  and without): Vendetta binds its own enemy for the fight (−1 turns, his seat, the mark beside it), no other enemy is
  taunted, and the log carries no Downwind line for it; **and the positive — a Mocking Blow's timed taunt is still carried**,
  read off its own Downwind line (the card taunts two bodies itself, so a count would not tell).
- **Snare Trap's snare.** **Driven** (`check_hv` §2c): the snare stands on its own body with his seat and no other; **with it
  spent, as its spring spends it, the Hunter can lay his next trap** — the arm that would have caught the copy holding his
  slot (HEAD's game reds it: H01); the snare **springs on its own body through the real turn** (the turn loop's own spring,
  its line in the log); and the spring's Poison — an affliction, laid by `_apply_poison` with him as its source — **is
  carried**: the wind leaves the trap, not what it lays.

### §3c — WHAT AN INDEX WOULD HAVE TO CARRY, PRICED

**A SUBJECT-level index saves almost nothing, on §3b's own figures.** If a batch opens a subject whenever it needs any of it,
the typical batch opens card, engine and rune law, usually combat and talent law too, beside the constitution and the method
it always reads — about 355–380 KiB of 433. The need is a scatter of blocks across the big subjects, so **only a BLOCK-level index can buy a real
saving**, and it has to carry, for each of 119 blocks:

1. **The rule as a sentence.** The headings already are one: **122 heading lines, 11.65 KiB.**
2. **Its TRIGGERS — the work that makes it bind**: the doors, fields and tables it governs, the kind of content it constrains
   (a rune, a card, a status, an engine, player-facing text) and the documents it rules. **A heading names the rule's shape, not
   when it applies**: *A SUBTRACTION IS OPEN AT THE BOTTOM WHERE AN ASSIGNMENT IS NOT* does not say *any rune or node that lowers a
   threshold*, and the batch about to author one is the reader who needs to be told. About 150 bytes a block: ~17 KiB.
3. **THE SETTLED QUESTIONS, listed by the question they close.** **25 blocks, 141,079 B (31.9%), carry an explicit guard against
   re-proposal** — *do not re-propose*, *do not re-open*, *do not re-derive*, *never "correct"*, *do not "restore"*. Their
   trigger is the batch's own idea, which no index of code nouns can anticipate, so they have to be indexed by the idea itself
   (*a cap on Loyalty*, *raising the end boss's discount*, *splitting the reasoning out*): ~3–5 KiB.

**The index prices at about 32–34 KiB**, and the always-read core beside it (the universal six) at 40 KiB: **about 73 KiB
read before any rule is opened, and 155–235 KiB for a batch with its blocks opened — about 192 on the nine's mean, a cut of
more than half from 433 KiB** (`recon3/index_core.py`).

**Too thin is a batch on a wrong premise, and the project already has the thin version and its scar**: the two indexes this
file carries for the instrument and combat splits are heading text alone (47 rows, 3,356 B; 11 rows, 635 B), and the INDEX
HAZARD recorded beside them — a pin satisfied by a heading row while the rule it names is gone — is the fault an index of
headings produces. Headings without triggers would also leave unrouted exactly the blocks §3b calls DEPENDED: **seven or eight a
batch, the ones neither the brief nor the report names, found here only by an agent that had read the whole file.**
**Too fat is the file again**: add each block's quoted rule (the blockquotes alone are 27.4 KiB) and its reason, and by about
100 KiB of index the core, the index and the opened blocks together approach the file they replace.

### §3d — THE FAILURE MODE, PRICED

**When a batch needs a block it did not open, it works from a premise it never read.** What would catch it, by kind of rule:

| a rule that is… | what catches a batch that never read it | share |
|---|---|---|
| **enforced by a behavioural gate** (the text sweeps, `check_gv` §0's sort of a new rune, `check_ek`'s list, a gate's drive) | **the battery** — a violation reds whether or not the rule was read. HP and HR each wrote *party* into player-facing strings and `test_batch_bx` §4b caught both, HP's in its pre-pass and HR's in its recon | the rule's real guard |
| **pinned in a gate** | the pin protects the rule's TEXT from deletion or rewording; it does not catch work that breaks the rule | 29 of 119 blocks |
| **a design rule or a settled question** (rune content is the designer's; a fold that moves a magnitude goes to the designer; *do not re-propose X*) | **nothing in the battery** — the designer reading the report, or nobody | 25 blocks carry an explicit guard (31.9%) |

**GY's reason belongs here, and an index model widens it.** GY rejected splitting the reasoning out because *the reasoning is
the thing that stops a batch re-proposing a settled question, and a batch only meets it by reading it* — and because it is *the
half no citation count can see*. **An index makes EVERY block on-demand, the reasoning and its rule alike**, so the settled
questions are exactly the blocks most at risk: cold until the batch that has the idea, and invisible to that batch unless the
index names the idea.

**But the nine batches also show that the full read does not prevent a wrong premise — it makes one checkable.**
- **Misses happened inside blocks a batch had open.** HL quoted the rune-scope block and missed its own clause that a rune
  whose required card only an engine brings is an `ENGINE_READ` row — so **Open Line** (it requires Guard Change, an enabler
  only the Stances bring since HL §1) has stood ungated for nine batches (verified at HEAD; §8). HR withheld a refused rune at the
  roll and missed FD §1's re-ask at a queued offer's answer (verified at HEAD: `Run.rune_choice` never asks `Runes.is_refused`).
- **Batches left stale claims in blocks they rewrote** (verified at HEAD): the rune-scope block still points at *THE PARTY
  SCOPE, AND THE TWO RUNES IT HOLDS* and says *two entries carry it* (HP renamed the crest block and five entries carry the
  scope); the drop block calls HL §1's rule *HO's rule* (HR wrote a brief's misattribution in); the governor table says Ruin
  *never clears* (the live Open Wound rune decays it a stack a turn).
- **Eight of the nine briefs carried at least one premise a rule in the file contradicted** (HQ's came from the file's own stale crest text) — HN's *a hero cannot un-fall
  mid-fight* against the revive block, HO's gating question already answered in the drop and crest blocks, HS's Tithe against
  its own read site, HT's *the Warrior has no half of anything* against the no-engine and kit blocks — **and nearly all were
  caught by the batch's premise table, by reading the rule in the file it had loaded.** Under an index the premise check works
  only if the index routes each premise to its block.

**What a miss costs is set by whether a gate stands behind the rule**: a red in the battery when one does (cheap — found before
the commit); a latent wrong build when none does (Open Line's missing row, nine batches); a ruling round-trip when the rule is a
settled question (HN's brief reasoning from the Bared Guard, retired since ET §1; HT's brief reaching for the reserved space's own word).

### §3f — THE RECOMMENDATION, AND THE ALTERNATIVE PRICED ANYWAY

**HN §1 ASKED WHETHER AN INDEX PLUS SUBJECTS READ ON DEMAND BEATS ONE FILE WITH A MOVING CEILING. THE ANSWER HAS THREE
PARTS.**
- **An index plus SUBJECTS does not.** A batch needs a thin share of nearly every large subject (§3b), so it would open card,
  rune and engine law, usually combat law, beside the constitution and the method: 355–380 KiB of 433. GR's test found no
  subject that batches read rarely, and §3b measures why.
- **An index plus BLOCKS does, on the read — by how much depends on what stays in the required read** (`recon3/index_core.py`,
  each index row priced at 250 B: its heading and its triggers).
  · **Everything but the universal six on demand**: 155–235 KiB a batch, about 192 on the nine's mean — more than half the read
    saved, and every settled question cold, which is GY's rejection in a larger form (§3d). **Not recommended.**
  · **The universal six, every block carrying a settled-question guard (the broad reading, 31 blocks, 167,256 B — §3c's 25 is
    the narrow one) and the eleven blocks seven or more of the nine needed stay in the required read**: a core of 37 blocks,
    190.7 KiB, and an index of 82 rows, about 20 KiB. **A batch then reads 229–297 KiB, 266 on the mean — about 38% less — and
    still meets GY's reasoning every time it reads the core. This is the shape the recon finds that beats one file.**
- **One file with a moving ceiling** is priced below; HN §1 ruled a fourth re-derivation is not the answer.

at once: a reference is missed when it is not opened (about nine of the instrument file's twelve misses), and it rots at its
seam wherever nothing mechanical holds it. An index makes 82 blocks references in one batch. **And it is a project, not a
batch**: 82 index rows with their triggers written by hand; the core's membership read by hand, because the guard's regex
under-reads (below, four of thirteen cold units close a question it does not catch); 97 pins and 36 targets re-pointed; and the
