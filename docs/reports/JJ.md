# Batch JJ — Malice, and what it pays for

**On `class-merge`, after JI (`3f74746`, JI's commit). IMPLEMENT ONLY.** `git ls-remote origin class-merge` read `3f74746`, JI's push and local HEAD, before anything ran (2026-11-04 10:36). **The designer's files were backed up first** (`../save-backups/JJ-20261104-103615`, byte-identical to JI's by `diff -r`) and read only through copies; md5s at the open and the close, §6.

**What shipped.** **The warband shares one pool, Malice.** It fills by a point for every 10 points of damage the heroes deal it, by 25 when one of its bodies falls, and by a point for every point a blow spends past a body's last, and it holds 150. **Its five heals pay for themselves out of it** — the Orc Totemist's and the Orc Shaman's Healing Wave, the Bog Troll's Regenerate, the Withered Warden's Wild Growth and the Grave Totem's Dark Vigil — a point of Malice for every point of health restored, so the warband can never heal more than its pool was given. A heal on a body under Blight the Well restores nothing, so it costs nothing. **The selection asks the pool where it asks the cooldown** (`_enemy_ready`), a declared heal sets its price aside, and **the healers heal whenever the pool allows and a body is hurt**. The pool stands in a strip above the warband's plates. Communion's chip counts how close the party is. Wretched Bellow cools 2 by hand. The state file's ceiling is 440 KiB, with its reason. JH's seven rulings and JI's five are answered and archived. `check_jj` is new; four closed gates are re-pointed, each at its own intent.

**IN PLAIN WORDS.** *The warband* is every enemy kind; *a heal* is one of its five healing moves. **The enemy side now has one shared bar, Malice, that fills as the heroes hurt it — a little for every hit, more for every kill, and all the damage wasted on an enemy that was already nearly dead. An enemy heal now costs that bar a point for every point of health it gives back**, so enemies can never heal more than the bar was given. Healers now heal whenever they can afford to and an ally is noticeably hurt. Over sixty paired runs the enemies healed less than before, not more, and later in each fight, because the bar is too low to pay for a heal early, when the healers used to heal for free; a healer that cannot afford a heal attacks instead. Fights got no longer, and not measurably harder.

**THE WORDS THIS REPORT USES.** *IMPLEMENT ONLY*: the brief's order to build what it names and author nothing else. *A ruling*: a choice that is the designer's; the batch takes its recommendation and lists it under NEEDS A RULING. *The routing*: the state file's index decides which of its blocks a batch reads while it works; the rest is read once at the close, by *the close-read*. *The blocks' reader*, *the new text's reader*, *the positional readers* and *the fresh reader*: agents outside the working read that check, at source, the blocks the routing opened, JJ's own new text, the 489 reads gates take by position, and this report. *A control*: a deliberate break of the tree that a gate must catch by name. *The battery*, *the pre-run* and *the acceptance*: every gate in the project, run on a copy and then in the repository. *`TUNED`*: `check_ji`'s table of cooldowns a designer set by hand. *GH*, *BL*, *HL §4* and the like: the batch that set a rule, named by its letters. *FOUND AT JJ*: the state file's list of things found and not fixed. *Paired*: the same seed on JI's tree and on JJ's, compared fight by fight while both runs stand on the same node; the run is the unit. *Two errors*: twice the standard error of a paired difference — inside it, a difference is a tie.

**Five findings matter most, and each moves something the brief said.**

- **THE POOL BINDS BY WHEN, NOT BY HOW MUCH.** The brief expected the pool at its cap for a good share of every fight, and fights longer and harder. Where a healer stands it sits at the cap for 16.0% of the turns and at 79.6% of the fights' ends — and it is too low to pay a heal in a fight's first half, where the warband used to heal for free: 19% of its heals fell in a fight's first half before, 6% now. So the warband heals less (18.5 → 13.2 health a fight over all fights; paired, −5.03 ± 1.94) and later, and **the fights are no longer and not measurably harder**: every outcome the brief named is a tie (JJ's RULINGS OWED 4). Nothing is tuned for it, as §4 ordered.
- **A HEAL's PRICE IS WHAT IT RESTORES ON THE BODY IT LANDS ON.** The brief's table priced each heal on JI's reference body; the data carries a share of a body, so a Healing Wave restores 20 on a Grave Totem and 75 on a Bog Troll. The rule is taken literally: priced at what it would restore now, paid at what the body gained (JJ's RULINGS OWED 1). Driven: healed equals spent in all 1,054 of JJ's fights. **And Blight the Well, a hero card that turns a heal on its bearer into damage, no longer waits on the pool**: the heal is asked for whole, as before JJ, and since it restores nothing the selection prices it at nothing — the new text's reader found the damage shrinking with the pool, and the fresh reader found the selection still keeping healers off a blighted body (§3).
- **A DECLARED HEAL SETS ITS PRICE ASIDE, OR JI's SUPPORT-FALLBACK DEFECT BECOMES REACHABLE.** Two healers declaring on one pool, a turn early, could both claim a heal it pays once, and the second would fall back to its basic aimed at its own ally (FOUND AT JI). The price rides the declaration, so re-validation asks no pool (JJ's RULINGS OWED 2).
- **THE BRIEF's PREMISE 1 WAS FALSE: A FIGHT IN FLIGHT DOES NOT SURVIVE A QUIT.** A quit fight restarts against the warband at full strength (GH), so the warband's pool restarts empty with it: nothing the warband or the battle holds rides the save (the party's losses do, GH).
- **THE COUNT DOES NOT RIDE THE `CD` CHIP's STRIP.** That chip is a display chip on an enemy's plate; a party's count belongs on the crest strip, the crest chip's home since HL §4, and it rides there beside Communion's chip (JJ's RULINGS OWED 5).

**THE VERDICT**: **DONE — GREEN BUT FOR THE TWO CARRIED REDS.** Malice ships as the brief built it: one pool for the warband, a point for every 10 points of the heroes' damage, 25 a kill and a point for every point of overkill, capped at 150, and every heal the warband casts pays a point of it for every point of health it restores; the healers heal whenever the pool allows and a body is hurt. **It taxes; it does not stall.** Over sixty paired runs at Warden the warband healed less (18.5 → 13.2 health a fight) and later, and **every outcome the brief named is a tie: fights are no longer, and not measurably harder.** **The pool binds by when, not by how much** — too low to pay a heal early, at its cap once the bodies that wanted healing are gone — and nothing is tuned for it (JJ's RULINGS OWED 4, the designer's). Communion's chip counts how close the party is, Wretched Bellow cools 2 by hand, and the state's ceiling is 440 KiB with its reason. Six rulings owed, each taken on the recommendation. The pre-run of record and the acceptance each read 175 targets with the two carried reds and nothing else; all twenty-two controls bite; all 489 positional reads hold; the drive's JJ arm is the replay on the code that lands.

## §3 — WHAT IT PAYS FOR

**A HEAL THE WARBAND CASTS COSTS A POINT OF MALICE FOR EVERY POINT OF HEALTH IT RESTORES.** The figures stay the handlers': a share of the body healed — 25% for Healing Wave and Regenerate, 20% for Wild Growth, 6% of every body for Dark Vigil — **lifted unchanged out of `_resolve_special`'s three arms into one door** (`_warband_heal_figure`) that the heal and its price both read; `check_ij` §3's four pins and `check_ji` §1a's three follow them there, needles unchanged. **The brief's table was JI's reference-body price** (31.9, 30.6, 75.0, 25.5, 31.9 — the blocks' reader recomputed each on the median composable body of 127.5); the data carries no amount, so the price is what the heal would restore on the body it lands on, and the cast pays what the body gained (JJ's RULINGS OWED 1). **The five are derived through the intent's own classifier, never listed**, and `check_jj` §3 drives each: priced at what it restores, paying it, no more than a pool of 7 can pay, and an overheal paying only what the bodies lacked.

**THE SPEND POLICY** (JJ's RULINGS OWED 3): a body is *hurt*, to a heal, while it is missing at least half of what the heal would restore it (`MALICE_HEAL_WORTH`). Healing Wave takes the most wounded hurt body, tanks and supports first; Regenerate itself; Wild Growth the most wounded, its coin gone; Dark Vigil two hurt bodies — the one heal whose condition tightened, HEAD's vigil firing on any two bodies below their maximum — each only while the pool pays. **Two of the five carry no cooldown** (the Shaman's Healing Wave and the Warden's Wild Growth), so the pool alone bounds them.

**BLIGHT THE WELL** (a Cleric card: a heal on its bearer becomes damage): the heal restores nothing, so it pays nothing and is asked for whole, and **the selection prices it at nothing**, so a low pool never keeps a healer off a blighted body and a declaration sets nothing aside for one. The selection reads no Blight, as it never did, so a short pool can leave a blighted body the one a healer can afford. `check_jj` §3 drives both halves, the cast and the choice; C04c and C04d red each.

**SELF-LIMITING, AND DRIVEN**: the warband's healing equals the Malice it spent in all 1,054 of JJ's fights, and no re-validation fell back on either arm. **You cannot be out-healed; you can only be taxed.** Whether a fight gets longer rather than harder is §4's.
