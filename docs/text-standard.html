DAWN OF DECAY — THE TEXT STANDARD

The rules every player-facing string is written to. Binding on every batch from CJ forward, including any tuning that comes out of playtesting. This document says how text is written; the audit report (text-audit.html) says where the current text breaks these rules. Neither one edits a description — the rewrite passes do that, scoped by the audit and governed by this.

0. What this governs, and the one rule above all the others

Every string a player can read: ability description and perfect_text, the twelve passive_desc blocks, class passive desc, status labels and chip tooltips, talent node text, rune and relic text, glossary entries, and the tables in master.html.

THE CODE'S FIELD IS AUTHORITATIVE. It is the only copy a player ever reads. master.html is corrected toward it, never the reverse. That is the project's standing rule and it governs this document too — where the two disagree, the document is wrong by definition, whatever it says and however well it says it.

There are two copies of nearly every description and they were written separately. Of the 120 draft ability rows in master.html, not one carries the code's sentence — every single one is an independently authored paraphrase, and the document's copy is a median 3.0x longer (118 of 120 rows are longer; the widest is 5.7x). They agree on the numbers — that was measured, see the audit — but they are not the same prose. Anyone calibrating "how the cards read" from master.html is calibrating from text no player has ever seen.

1. THE VALUE ON THE LINE — the load-bearing rule

A percentage is followed by its computed value in parentheses: 20% of maximum health (34).

THE PARENTHETICAL IS COMPUTED AT RENDER TIME AND IS NEVER AUTHORED INTO A STRING. The description holds a TOKEN and the renderer expands it (Classes.resolve_values, Batch CL §1). A literal digit inside parentheses in any authored field is a defect. Typing the number in would create two hundred new places for a value to drift out of sync with the code — the exact failure text-audit.html exists to catch, reintroduced at scale.

THIS SUPERSEDES THE TWO-TIER ARITHMETIC SPLIT that stood here until Batch CL. The tiers existed because mid-combat surfaces could not show arithmetic. With the value resolved on the line the player is never asked to compute anything, and the formula stays visible so scaling stays legible — so the split has nothing left to do. It is rewritten rather than appended to, because a superseded rule left standing beside its replacement is how BY's "quote only gates 1 and 3" survived twelve batches.

1.1 The four resolution cases

  • A percentage that is already final resolves to the resulting TOTAL, on chips only. +25% Block chance (→ 35%) on a status chip, because mid-fight the useful number is what Block actually is now. Everywhere else a final percentage stands alone — a card reading +25% Block chance (25%) is noise.
  • Volatile stats resolve live and are allowed to move. 20% of CURRENT health (28) will read differently next turn and that is correct — it is right at the moment it is read, and tooltips are re-read every time they open. Do not freeze, snapshot or mark them.
  • Where there is nothing to resolve against, the percentage stands alone. Glossary entries have no hero; 20% of the target's maximum health on a draft card has no target. No parenthetical, no placeholder, no dash. The resolver treats an absent base as a legal answer, which is why it cannot fail.
  • Resolve against the owner the text names. The token carries the owner and the renderer supplies the words, so §4.2's name-the-owner rule and the base the value divides into are ONE edit and cannot drift apart. A self-owned value is bare ("maximum health"); an other-owned one says whose.

1.2 What each surface can resolve against

Which surface a string lands on is not a matter of opinion. One ability description is rendered by seven screens and they do not all hold a hero. Every one of them calls the resolver — a surface with nothing to resolve against still has to expand its tokens, or a raw {mhp:20} reaches a label.

The resolver is for values the renderer does NOT already print on its own line.

2. DIRECTNESS

State the effect, then the Perfect variant. Stop.

NO DESIGN RATIONALE ON A CARD, EVER. This is the dominant fluff pattern and it is everywhere, because these strings were written by whoever was justifying the decision at the time. The example to calibrate against — Blood Offering, as master.html read when this standard was written at Batch CJ (Batch CN has since taken Blood Offering's skill check away and folded its Perfect into the base, so the live rows no longer carry the parenthetical; the example is kept verbatim because it is about the SECOND SENTENCE, and that fault is unchanged):

He loses 20% of his CURRENT health and gains 40 Rage (Perfect: 60). Because it is a share of what is left rather than of his maximum, it can never take him below 1 — and it costs less the lower he is.

The second sentence explains why the number was chosen. That belongs in the changelog. The card should read:

Loses 20% of current health. Gains 60 Rage.

The code's card for the same ability is a different string, and it is already much closer to the target — which is the shape of the whole problem:

Open a vein on purpose: lose 20% of your CURRENT health and gain 60 Rage. It can never take you below 1 — and the lower you are, the less it costs.

It still breaks three rules below (second person, a rationale clause, a flavor opener carrying mechanics), but it is two sentences rather than three and it does not lecture. The rewrite passes are correcting the code's string, not the document's.

3. FLAVOR

Allowed, and welcome where a card earns it. One short clause, mechanically empty, and it must not restate what the mechanics line already said. Never a sentence explaining how or why the ability works. If it could be mistaken for a rule, cut it.

Two failure modes, both currently present:

  • Flavor that carries the mechanics. "Open a vein on purpose:" is the sentence's subject — cut it and the sentence loses its verb. Flavor must be removable.
  • Flavor instead of mechanics. Aimed Shot reads, in full: "A perfect line. Patient, precise, final." It deals damage, builds Focus and grants +20 Focus on a Perfect (Aimed Shot deals damage, so Batch CN left its check alone), and the card says none of it. On the draft screen, where no computed line follows, that card is blank.

"Perfect" is a mechanic and the word is reserved. Aimed Shot's "A perfect line" is flavor colliding with the keyword on a card that also has a Perfect bonus.

4. THE CONSISTENCY RULES

4.1 Person — no pronouns

"Loses 20% of current health", not "he loses" and not "you lose". Pick this once and it never drifts again.

Currently the field is split in both directions: 86 of 204 ability descriptions use second person ("you", "your") and 44 use third person ("he", "his", "her"). Some use both. master.html is uniformly third-person, so the document and the card disagree on voice for most of the game.

4.2 Whose percentage — always name the owner

"20% of his maximum health" and "20% of the target's maximum health" are different abilities and currently read the same. Name the owner every time, even when it seems obvious — it is only obvious to whoever wrote it.

4.3 Perfect — one notation everywhere

(Perfect: X), and X is the changed value, not a sentence about it.

The rendered label is already uniform, because every surface that shows it supplies the words "Perfect: " itself. The inconsistency is inside the field — 190 perfect_text strings in at least four shapes: a bare value ("+10 bonus Mana"), an "instead" comparison ("60 Rage instead of 40", 107 of them), a full sentence ("The debt pays 35% of maximum health instead of 25%"), and a compound ("+20% parry instead of +15%, and holds 6 turns instead of 4").

The value alone is the standard: the base is on the line above, so "instead of 40" repeats it. Two other faults to fix while passing through:

  • Never state the Perfect condition inside the description as well. Four abilities do (Deadfall, Pommel Strike, Aimed Shot, Snare Trap) — the tooltip then says it twice.
  • The draft card shows no Perfect at all. A card is drafted on a screen that never mentions its Perfect bonus. That is a code gap, not a text gap — recorded in the audit and owed to a later batch.

4.4 Keywords capitalised

Mechanics take a capital so the player can tell the mechanic from the ordinary word: Break, Broken, Block, Parry, Perfect, Chilled, Frozen, Burn, Ruin, Faith, Mercy, Rage, Focus, Loyalty, Resonance, Empower.

Break is the one that matters most — "break" as a verb appears in ordinary prose and reads as the mechanic. The ability corpus is currently clean on this (no lowercase "break" in any of the 204), and it is written down so it stays that way.

Damage against the Break meter is "Break damage (BD)" everywhere — the project's existing term, unchanged.

4.5 Duration vs cooldown — never a bare "3 turns"

Write "for 3 turns" for a duration and "3-turn cooldown" for a cooldown, and never let the bare number stand for either. 97 of 204 descriptions currently carry a bare "N turns" with nothing distinguishing which is meant.

The tooltip prints its own Cooldown: N turns line, so a description restating the cooldown is both a second copy and a chance to disagree. Descriptions state durations; the renderer states cooldowns.

4.5a A DURATION IS STATED AS APPLIED (BINDING, SET AT BATCH CV §1)

"N turns" is the number the code passes to _apply_status — the CALENDAR count. It is never the acting-turn count. tick_statuses() decrements at the START of a unit's turn, so a status applied with 4 covers four calendar turns and three of the bearer's own actions. Write 4.

THE CHIP DECIDES IT. The chip counts down the raw value, so a node that says 3 while the chip shows 4 contradicts something the player is looking at. The acting-turn reading also makes every description a TRANSLATION of its own code — and translations rot, which is how the corpus came to hold both readings at once, cr_rime using both in one sentence. tick_statuses() running before a unit acts is an implementation detail and never a wording convention.

4.6 Stacking and refresh — state what a second cast does

Whenever a second cast is possible, say what it does: refresh, extend, stack, or replace. This is currently absent almost everywhere — of 61 abilities that grant a timed effect, 53 say nothing about recasting.

4.7 No literal \n in a prose field — WITH ONE MEASURED EXCEPTION

The rule is right and the reason it cannot be applied everywhere at once is worth stating, because applying it blindly would break the battle tooltip.

Nothing in the project overrides make_custom_tooltip, so every tooltip is Godot's default — and Godot's default tooltip does not autowrap. It grows to fit the longest line. The authored \n in an ability description is therefore load-bearing on the battle button, the hero sheet and the rune slot: strip it and a 322-character description renders as one line roughly two thousand pixels wide.

  • passive_desc — the rule applies AT THE RENDERER, NOT IN THE FIELD, and Batch CL found out why. It renders in an autowrapping 400px label on the hero sheet, where the authored breaks fight the soft wrap instead of helping it: 23 of its 67 lines are wider than the box that holds them (measured, up to 71 characters against a 61-character budget), across five specs — Arcanist, Holy, Inquisitor, Occultist and Beastmaster. That finding is real and it is fixed. But this field has a THIRD surface that CJ's audit and CL's brief both missed: battle.gd:1086 hands the same string to add_status, and unit.gd:2291 makes that a chip tooltip_text — a Godot default tooltip, which does not autowrap either. Deleting the breaks from the field would tidy the hero sheet and render the Arcanist's block as one ~2000px line in the middle of a fight, which is the exact failure this section records for ability descriptions. So the breaks STAY in the field and are flattened by the two surfaces that soft-wrap — the hero sheet's label and the spec-choice column — which is the pattern spec_choice_screen already used for ability descriptions.
  • Ability description — the breaks stay until there is an autowrapping tooltip. Removing them is a code batch (a custom tooltip), not a text batch. Until then the hand-wrap is what keeps the tooltip readable, and it is held to the ceiling below.

One inconsistency to fix on the way past: spec_choice_screen.gd flattens \n to a space for ability descriptions and does not do the same for passive_desc two expressions later, so the passive block hard-wraps inside a 298px column on that screen.

4.8 The length ceiling — 44 characters, and it was measured

An authored line may not exceed 44 characters.

Measured, not guessed, against the real font at the real sizes the screens set. The narrowest surface that renders a description is the draft card: 258px wide at font size 11 (DRAFT_COL_W 296 less 38). The default theme font is Open Sans SemiBold. Across all 1,846 authored lines in the game:

  • Every observed line of 44 characters or fewer fits. The widest 44-character line measured 241px against a 258px budget.
  • 45 is the first length at which a line overflows — 263px, over budget.
  • The ceiling is a character count rather than a pixel count because the prose uses ALL-CAPS runs for emphasis, and caps are wider: 44 mixed-case characters fit comfortably, but 44 capitals would not. 44 holds for both because no authored line is all capitals.

The ability corpus already holds this ceiling perfectly — 936 of 936 lines, zero overflows. The existing hand-wrapping discipline is correct and the ceiling is written down to record it, not to change it. Everything that overflows is a field that does not carry authored breaks at all (talent and rune text, which autowrap and are fine) or passive_desc (§4.7).

BATCH CL MEASURED WHAT THE PARENTHETICAL COST, and the answer is nothing. §1 warned that resolving a value adds five to seven characters after the hand-wrapping is already done, and told the batch to ship, measure, and then fix only what overflowed. Measured over the whole corpus with tokens expanded against all four class bases (check_cl_width.gd): 3,864 rendered description lines, zero over the ceiling, and exactly one perfect_text line pushed over by a parenthetical — Pyre Wake, at 47 characters — which was tightened in place. No rewrap was needed and none was done.

One thing the measurement corrected: the 936-of-936 result is about description only, and perfect_text has never been held to this ceiling. 76 of its 788 rendered lines are over 44 characters and all 76 pre-date Batch CL (the widest is Feint's, at 67). That is not the defect it looks like: the draft card's computed block autowraps, so the ceiling does not bind there, and the only surface that renders perfect_text without wrapping is the battle tooltip, which grows to fit rather than clipping. Recorded as measured, not filed as owed — rewrapping 76 lines to a ceiling that does not bind them would be work with no reader.

4.9 HERO AND ALLY ARE NOT SYNONYMS (BINDING, SET AT BATCH CV §4)

HERO — the four party members. ALLY — heroes and companions together.

CORRECTED AT BATCH DJ §1 — the beast does NOT stand in heroes. It is appended to companions; heroes.append is reached at exactly one site, the party spawn. The beast is visibly on the player's side and is skipped by is_companion filters throughout battle.gd — but those filters are reading an array that never held one, so the collection is what excludes, not the filter. Twenty-five node texts promised "every ally" or "the whole party" and paid only the four. The exclusion is intended — under The Pack a party-wide buff would otherwise reach six units — so the words move, never the read site.

THE TEST IS THE READ SITE'S COLLECTION AND THEN THE CHAIN BELOW IT, NOT ITS FILTER AND NOT THE SENTENCE'S RHYTHM. _hero_side() includes companions (the living ones), so an ability that targets through it says ally. Any walk over bare heroes does not — with or without a not h.is_companion clause — so it says hero. _gain_faith refuses companions outright, so nothing Faith-flavoured can ever say ally; and a per-TURN effect can never reach one either, because _next_unit() walks heroes + enemies and a beast takes no turns. "Party" is not one of the two words; it reads as either and is what let twenty-five texts drift.

AND A THIRD FAILURE, FOUND AT BATCH DK: THE COLLECTION CAN REACH A BEAST AND THE EFFECT STILL NOT ARRIVE. wd_tank_spank was widened, measured, and reverted to hero: empower attaches to a companion cleanly — chip, tooltip and all — and pays it nothing, because a beast's blows resolve through _companion_hit, which reads none of the hero strike loop's multipliers. Measured over 40 seeded blows, ratio exactly 1.0000. So the evidence for ally is a live measurement on a summoned beast, not an assertion about a collection.

Precision runs both directions, and Batch CV's four were moved on a test that was never run. wd_rally, wd_hold_line, dv_devoutness and dv_waters were moved TO ally because "their read sites genuinely include companions". None of the four did (DJ §2). DK ruled on all four and they split two and two: wd_rally and wd_hold_line had their CODE corrected and are ally truthfully now; dv_devoutness and dv_waters had their TEXT corrected to hero, the first because it is stamped at party spawn before a beast exists and the second because it is per-turn. All four land correctly for the first time, by a different route than CV recorded.

THE LIVE SPLIT, AS OF DK. Five effects say ally and mean it: Rally, Hold the Line, Sanctuary, the Field Medic's cleanses and Harvest. Everything else party-wide says hero, and each carries its reason in the source beside it — "hero" alone is a decision the next author re-litigates.

5. HOW TO APPLY THIS

  1. Fix WRONG before writing prose. Polishing the grammar on a wrong description makes it read more confidently while staying wrong, which is worse than leaving it clumsy. The audit's bucket 1 is the queue.
  2. Edit the code's field. Then correct master.html toward it. Never the reverse, and never only one of the two.
  3. Rationale goes to changelog.html, never onto the card. If a sentence explains a choice, it has a home and this is not it.
  4. Check the surface before allowing arithmetic (§1). The same field is read by a screen that computes numbers for you and a screen that does not.
