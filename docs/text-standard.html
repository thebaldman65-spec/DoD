DAWN OF DECAY — THE TEXT STANDARD

The rules every player-facing string is written to. Binding on every batch from CJ forward, including any tuning that comes out of playtesting. This document says how text is written; the audit report (text-audit.html) says where the current text breaks these rules. Neither one edits a description — the rewrite passes do that, scoped by the audit and governed by this.

0. What this governs, and the one rule above all the others

Every string a player can read: ability description and perfect_text, the twelve passive_desc blocks, class passive desc, status labels and chip tooltips, talent node text, rune and relic text, glossary entries, and the tables in master.html.

THE CODE'S FIELD IS AUTHORITATIVE. It is the only copy a player ever reads. master.html is corrected toward it, never the reverse. That is the project's standing rule and it governs this document too — where the two disagree, the document is wrong by definition, whatever it says and however well it says it.

There are two copies of nearly every description and they were written separately. Of the 120 draft ability rows in master.html, not one carries the code's sentence — every single one is an independently authored paraphrase, and the document's copy is a median 3.0x longer (118 of 120 rows are longer; the widest is 5.7x). They agree on the numbers — that was measured, see the audit — but they are not the same prose. Anyone calibrating "how the cards read" from master.html is calibrating from text no player has ever seen.

1. THE TWO TIERS — the load-bearing rule

DRAFT SCREEN AND GLOSSARY — percentages and formulas are allowed. This is where the player is comparing cards and wants the arithmetic available.

EVERYWHERE ELSE, ESPECIALLY MID-COMBAT — no arithmetic. A real computed number where one is available at tooltip time; otherwise plain direct language. The player must never be asked to do math while choosing an action.

Where a live value is cheap to compute from data already at hand, use it. Where it is not, use the formula and leave a note in the batch that writes it — the live-value version is a later batch.

Which surface a string lands on is not a matter of opinion, and the tiers cut across a single field. One ability description is rendered by five different screens, and only some of them are the draft:

SurfaceShowsTier
Draft card (map_screen.gd)description ONLY — no computed damage, no cost, no cooldown, no PerfectArithmetic allowed
Blacksmith commission (blacksmith_screen.gd)description + live damage + scaling + PerfectArithmetic allowed
Battle button tooltip (battle.gd)description + live damage/BD + cooldown + initiative + PerfectNO arithmetic
Hero sheet (party_screen.gd)description + Perfect; passive_desc in its own blockNO arithmetic
Offer / drop picker (map_screen.gd)description ONLY NO arithmetic

The battle tooltip already computes the real numbers — damage range against the unit's current Attack, Break damage, cooldown with turns remaining, initiative cost, and the Perfect line. A description that also states "25% of Attack" is stating in arithmetic what the line beneath it states as a number. The live value wins; the formula goes.

2. DIRECTNESS

State the effect, then the Perfect variant. Stop.

NO DESIGN RATIONALE ON A CARD, EVER. This is the dominant fluff pattern and it is everywhere, because these strings were written by whoever was justifying the decision at the time. The example to calibrate against — Blood Offering, as master.html currently reads:

He loses 20% of his CURRENT health and gains 40 Rage (Perfect: 60). Because it is a share of what is left rather than of his maximum, it can never take him below 1 — and it costs less the lower he is.

The second sentence explains why the number was chosen. That belongs in the changelog. The card should read:

Loses 20% of current health. Gains 40 Rage (Perfect: 60).

The code's card for the same ability is a different string, and it is already much closer to the target — which is the shape of the whole problem:

Open a vein on purpose: lose 20% of your CURRENT health and gain 40 Rage. It can never take you below 1 — and the lower you are, the less it costs.

It still breaks three rules below (second person, a rationale clause, a flavor opener carrying mechanics), but it is two sentences rather than three and it does not lecture. The rewrite passes are correcting the code's string, not the document's.

3. FLAVOR

Allowed, and welcome where a card earns it. One short clause, mechanically empty, and it must not restate what the mechanics line already said. Never a sentence explaining how or why the ability works. If it could be mistaken for a rule, cut it.

Two failure modes, both currently present:

  • Flavor that carries the mechanics. "Open a vein on purpose:" is the sentence's subject — cut it and the sentence loses its verb. Flavor must be removable.
  • Flavor instead of mechanics. Aimed Shot reads, in full: "A perfect line. Patient, precise, final." It deals damage, builds Focus and grants +20 Focus on a Perfect, and the card says none of it. On the draft screen, where no computed line follows, that card is blank.

"Perfect" is a mechanic and the word is reserved. Aimed Shot's "A perfect line" is flavor colliding with the keyword on a card that also has a Perfect bonus.

4. THE CONSISTENCY RULES

4.1 Person — no pronouns

"Loses 20% of current health", not "he loses" and not "you lose". Pick this once and it never drifts again.

Currently the field is split in both directions: 86 of 204 ability descriptions use second person ("you", "your") and 44 use third person ("he", "his", "her"). Some use both. master.html is uniformly third-person, so the document and the card disagree on voice for most of the game.

4.2 Whose percentage — always name the owner

"20% of his maximum health" and "20% of the target's maximum health" are different abilities and currently read the same. Name the owner every time, even when it seems obvious — it is only obvious to whoever wrote it.

4.3 Perfect — one notation everywhere

(Perfect: X), and X is the changed value, not a sentence about it.

The rendered label is already uniform, because every surface that shows it supplies the words "Perfect: " itself. The inconsistency is inside the field — 190 perfect_text strings in at least four shapes: a bare value ("+10 bonus Mana"), an "instead" comparison ("60 Rage instead of 40", 107 of them), a full sentence ("The debt pays 35% of maximum health instead of 25%"), and a compound ("+20% parry instead of +15%, and holds 6 turns instead of 4").

The value alone is the standard: the base is on the line above, so "instead of 40" repeats it. Two other faults to fix while passing through:

  • Never state the Perfect condition inside the description as well. Four abilities do (Deadfall, Pommel Strike, Aimed Shot, Snare Trap) — the tooltip then says it twice.
  • The draft card shows no Perfect at all. A card is drafted on a screen that never mentions its Perfect bonus. That is a code gap, not a text gap — recorded in the audit and owed to a later batch.

4.4 Keywords capitalised

Mechanics take a capital so the player can tell the mechanic from the ordinary word: Break, Broken, Block, Parry, Perfect, Chilled, Frozen, Burn, Ruin, Faith, Mercy, Rage, Focus, Loyalty, Resonance, Empower.

Break is the one that matters most — "break" as a verb appears in ordinary prose and reads as the mechanic. The ability corpus is currently clean on this (no lowercase "break" in any of the 204), and it is written down so it stays that way.

Damage against the Break meter is "Break damage (BD)" everywhere — the project's existing term, unchanged.

4.5 Duration vs cooldown — never a bare "3 turns"

Write "for 3 turns" for a duration and "3-turn cooldown" for a cooldown, and never let the bare number stand for either. 97 of 204 descriptions currently carry a bare "N turns" with nothing distinguishing which is meant.

The tooltip prints its own Cooldown: N turns line, so a description restating the cooldown is both a second copy and a chance to disagree. Descriptions state durations; the renderer states cooldowns.

4.6 Stacking and refresh — state what a second cast does

Whenever a second cast is possible, say what it does: refresh, extend, stack, or replace. This is currently absent almost everywhere — of 61 abilities that grant a timed effect, 53 say nothing about recasting.

4.7 No literal \n in a prose field — WITH ONE MEASURED EXCEPTION

The rule is right and the reason it cannot be applied everywhere at once is worth stating, because applying it blindly would break the battle tooltip.

Nothing in the project overrides make_custom_tooltip, so every tooltip is Godot's default — and Godot's default tooltip does not autowrap. It grows to fit the longest line. The authored \n in an ability description is therefore load-bearing on the battle button, the hero sheet and the rune slot: strip it and a 322-character description renders as one line roughly two thousand pixels wide.

  • passive_desc — the rule applies now. It renders in an autowrapping 400px label on the hero sheet, where the authored breaks fight the soft wrap instead of helping it. 23 of its 67 lines are wider than the box that holds them (measured, up to 71 characters against a 61-character budget), across five specs — Arcanist, Holy, Inquisitor, Occultist and Beastmaster. This is the "wraps badly at different widths" case, and it is real.
  • Ability description — the breaks stay until there is an autowrapping tooltip. Removing them is a code batch (a custom tooltip), not a text batch. Until then the hand-wrap is what keeps the tooltip readable, and it is held to the ceiling below.

One inconsistency to fix on the way past: spec_choice_screen.gd flattens \n to a space for ability descriptions and does not do the same for passive_desc two expressions later, so the passive block hard-wraps inside a 298px column on that screen.

4.8 The length ceiling — 44 characters, and it was measured

An authored line may not exceed 44 characters.

Measured, not guessed, against the real font at the real sizes the screens set. The narrowest surface that renders a description is the draft card: 258px wide at font size 11 (DRAFT_COL_W 296 less 38). The default theme font is Open Sans SemiBold. Across all 1,846 authored lines in the game:

  • Every observed line of 44 characters or fewer fits. The widest 44-character line measured 241px against a 258px budget.
  • 45 is the first length at which a line overflows — 263px, over budget.
  • The ceiling is a character count rather than a pixel count because the prose uses ALL-CAPS runs for emphasis, and caps are wider: 44 mixed-case characters fit comfortably, but 44 capitals would not. 44 holds for both because no authored line is all capitals.

The ability corpus already holds this ceiling perfectly — 936 of 936 lines, zero overflows. The existing hand-wrapping discipline is correct and the ceiling is written down to record it, not to change it. Everything that overflows is a field that does not carry authored breaks at all (talent and rune text, which autowrap and are fine) or passive_desc (§4.7).

5. HOW TO APPLY THIS

  1. Fix WRONG before writing prose. Polishing the grammar on a wrong description makes it read more confidently while staying wrong, which is worse than leaving it clumsy. The audit's bucket 1 is the queue.
  2. Edit the code's field. Then correct master.html toward it. Never the reverse, and never only one of the two.
  3. Rationale goes to changelog.html, never onto the card. If a sentence explains a choice, it has a home and this is not it.
  4. Check the surface before allowing arithmetic (§1). The same field is read by a screen that computes numbers for you and a screen that does not.
