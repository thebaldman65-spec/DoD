§0 — COVERAGE, STATED HONESTLY

CJ's audit reported 120 of 120 stat cells clean and was later found to have had no check for stated bonuses with no code path at all. An audit that overstates its own reach is worse than a narrow one that admits its edges, so this section is deliberately specific about what was NOT done.

What every one of the 324 got

  • Extraction from the live trees, not a hand-parse. A throwaway Godot script walked Talents.LANE_TREES and dumped all 324 with their rendered text (through Talents.desc_for, the same function the tooltip calls), payload, scale, granted_name and collision_kind. 12 specs × 27 cells = 324, confirmed.
  • A read site, opened. Every distinct stat field a payload writes (319 of them) was resolved to at least one line of shipped code that is not a comment and not the unit.gd declaration. Comment-only mentions were rejected as read sites — a field whose only appearance outside talents.gd is prose would be dead, and none are.
  • A magnitude comparison. Every number in every node's rendered text was compared against the numbers present in the payload and within ±8 non-comment lines of every read site, with unit conversions (0.12↔12, 1.15↔15, 0.60↔40) applied both ways.
  • The three targeted sweeps in the table above: base-quoting, Perfect-claiming, party-scope.

What only 68 of the 324 got — AND THIS IS THE GAP

Line-by-line semantic reading — the pass that catches caps, silent exclusions, conditions, interactions and "whose percentage is it" — was done node-by-node for berserker (27), swordmaster (27) and warden (14 of 27). That is 68 of 324. Every bucket-2 finding below except the scope sweep came out of those 68, which is the honest reason to expect more in the other nine specs: the yield was roughly one INCOMPLETE per ten nodes read, and 256 nodes have not been read that way.

The Mystic is the thinnest spot in this document. It produced zero hits in every automated pass, so not one of its 27 nodes was hand-opened. Zero flags is real evidence about magnitudes and nothing at all about caps or scope. Cryomancer, arcanist, holy, inquisitor, sharpshooter, beastmaster, occultist and pyromancer each had only their flagged nodes opened (between 1 and 7 apiece).

Two things nothing here checked at all: whether a node's magnitude is well balanced (out of scope — that is the work this audit exists to make possible), and whether the tooltip surfaces that re-render node text agree with it. One such disagreement was found by accident (§4, the Blood Frenzy floor) and there is no reason to think it is alone.

1.2 — dv_stalwart "Stalwart" — BOTH numbers wrong

inquisitor / Bulwark / row 5. Text: "Divine Shield absorbs 50% of the Devout's max health (up from the base 30%)." Payload: {"stalwart_step": 20}.

Two read sites agree with each other and disagree with the text: battle.gd:5431 (the intent preview) and battle.gd:14502 (the cast) both compute 0.35 + 0.01 * stalwart_step. At stalwart_step = 20 that is 0.55 — the shield absorbs 55%, not 50% — and the base it is added to is 35%, not 30%.

The cause is on the record. CQ's fold audit row for Divine Shield (changelog.html:906) reads base "absorbs 30%", perfect "absorbs 35%"; CN took the bar off, so the perfect's number became the base, and CQ's own summary states it plainly at changelog.html:1450: "Divine Shield absorbs 35% instead of 30%". Divine Shield was not among CR's three duration reverts. The ability moved and the talent quoting it did not.

The read site's own comment is a third answer. battle.gd:14498 still says "Absorbs 30% (perfect 35%)" directly above a line that does 35% unconditionally with no perfect gate at all. Three statements of one number, no two alike.

§3 — NO READ SITE, AND UNREACHABLE

NO READ SITE — none at field level, and that is a real result. All 319 distinct stat fields the 324 payloads write have at least one live, non-comment read site. Comment-only mentions were explicitly rejected, so this is not the weaker claim that the name appears somewhere. The four dead clauses in §1.1 are the functional equivalent and are filed there because they are dead for a reason the designer needs to see.

UNREACHABLE — no node is ungrantable. All 324 were enumerated from the trees, so none is orphaned by a rename or absent from a tree; all 12 specs are reachable; every payload condition (has_node, owns_ability) names a node or ability that exists.

The reverse case does exist, and is reported because it is the shape a later batch will misread. Four fields have live read sites in battle.gd and are written by no node at all — seeding_ranks, emp_frostbolt_ranks, frost_ward_ranks, freezing_ranks. Each is commented "no node:" in unit.gd, so this is known and deliberate; the nodes that once wrote them (py_seeding, cr_emp_frostbolt, cr_frost_ward, cr_freezing) were re-specced onto different fields and their texts are correct against the NEW fields. Dead code, not a dead node — listed so the next reader does not trace a node to the wrong site.

§8.6 — SAVED ALLOCATIONS: WHERE THEY LIVE, AND WHAT A RESTRUCTURE DOES TO THEM

Measured by check_dn.gd against a scratch profile, not reasoned about. Profile.save_path is a var precisely so a headless check can redirect it.

  • Profile.talent_cells — spec → {node id: true}. The permanent unlock ledger. Keyed by NODE ID.
  • Profile.talent_equipped — spec → {row (as String): node id}. Keyed by ROW NUMBER. One node per row is enforced by the KEY, not by a check.
  • Profile.talent_points — spec → points EARNED, ever. There is no second accounting: available = earned − Talents.cells_spent(tree, cells), and cells_spent prices each owned cell off the row it currently sits in.
  • Profile.VERSION is 2 and THE LOAD IS TOLERANT — keys are merged over defaults and data["version"] = VERSION is stamped unconditionally. There is no migration step anywhere in the file, and no version a restructure could bump to trigger one.

THE MEASUREMENT. A Berserker with all 27 cells bought (spent 54 of 54, available 0), then three edits a restructure makes:

The editspentavailableWhat the player sees

One row-1 node MOVED to row 9 (tier 1 → tier 3, 1 point → 3) 56−2A NEGATIVE PURSE. Nothing refuses it, nothing clamps it, nothing logs it. The player keeps every cell and simply cannot buy again until they refund something.

NOTHING ABOVE THROWS AND version IS STAMPED 2 EITHER WAY. That is the whole danger: meta progression is the one thing in this game that cannot be re-earned quickly (1 point per spec per ZONE BOSS), and every failure mode here is silent.

What a migration would have to do, given the above:

  1. Bump Profile.VERSION to 3 — there is currently nothing to hang a migration on, because a tolerant merge cannot tell a v2 save from a v3 one.
  2. Re-price, not re-key. Ids are stable across a re-sort (the trees' own convention since Batch AJ is "every node keeps its id so saved trees migrate"). The rows are what move, and the row IS the price. A migration must recompute cells_spent under the NEW rows and grant or reclaim the difference explicitly.
  3. Rebuild talent_equipped from its VALUES. Its keys are stale row numbers the moment a node changes row. equipped_talents() already drops ids the tree no longer holds, so a DELETION is survivable; a MOVE is not, because the id validates fine under the wrong row key.
  4. Decide what happens to a node that is CUT. Today its point is refunded by accident, as a side effect of cells_spent skipping unknown ids. That is the right outcome reached by the wrong mechanism, and it should be made deliberate.

Two node ids cannot be renamed without moving the save format regardless: bm_beast_within and bm_no_beast_left, already on the standing list in docs/state.md.

§8.8 — THE HONEST ANSWER

THIS IS NOT A RE-SORT AND A RENAME. IT IS A RE-AUTHOR OF ROUGHLY A THIRD OF THE TALENT LAYER, AND THE DESIGNER SHOULD KNOW THAT BEFORE STARTING.

The two halves of the audit disagree, and that is the finding.

  • THE CONTENT IS ALMOST CLEAN. Of 324 nodes, six depend on something drawn, and only two are dead without the draw. 318 need no charter-driven change at all under §1's reading. The trees were purpose-authored against their specs' own mechanics and it shows.
  • THE SHAPE CANNOT BE BUILT FROM THEM. Zero of twelve specs can fill three nine-cell lanes. 97 new nodes — 30% of the layer — would have to be written, and two specs hold a lane at zero, which no shrinking of the tree can rescue.

Which means the cost is not in the 324. It is in the 97. And 97 new nodes is a larger authoring job than the original twelve-tree pass, because those were written to a THEME the spec already had, and these must be written to a CATEGORY the spec demonstrably has no material for — nine offensive nodes for a Cleric who has never had one, nine defensive nodes for a Sharpshooter who has never had one.

Three things fall out of that, and none of them is a ruling:

  1. The charter's own §0 and §1 disagree about 75 nodes (§8.1.1). Settle that sentence before anything else, because it is the difference between 235 clean and 318 clean.
  2. The saved-allocation risk is real, silent, and cheap to fix (§8.6) — but only if it is fixed BEFORE the restructure ships, not after. There is currently no version a migration could hang on.
  3. A smaller tree is worth considering on its own merits and does not solve this one. At four rows per lane the shortfall falls from 97 to 22 and four specs fill — a real improvement, and still 22 nodes and eight specs short.
