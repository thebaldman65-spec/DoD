2026-10-01 — Batch HQ: ONE MAGNITUDE, ONE KEY, AND THE WORD THE TAXONOMY IS MISSING

Dead Air now pays 75% more damage while no Mage stands, where it paid 50%. A crest rune asking whether a standing hero carries a core rune is now asked again every time a hero falls or is raised, like every other question about who stands. The talent Breaking Heals a Hero says what it actually pays. A rune that pays only while a condition holds has a name in the rune taxonomy, conditional. And two checks that printed the wrong shop door now print the Peddler's. Full working: docs/reports/HQ.md.

§0 — HP'S FIVE RULINGS. Dirge stays at 45% and Empty Pulpit at 50%, as HP proposed them. Dead Air goes to 75%: the Mage deals a bit under half of the four heroes' damage, so the three left standing need about three quarters more to do the work of four, and at 50% he was only half replaced. Dirge stands on the same logic — three heroes at 1.45 deal more than four at 1.00 — and it is recorded that it farms nothing: a fallen hero cannot act or heal and makes the fight longer, and measured, Dirge made heroes fall less and carried runs deeper. Empty Pulpit's small share of a won fight's damage is recorded as something to watch in play, not a fault. Fellowship's retirement stands exactly as HP left it: a save that held it loads with it, and the line in HP's brief that said otherwise was the brief's mistake, not a reversal. The core-rune question joins the questions read as the fight runs. Tithe's words stand and the talent's are narrowed. And the battle log's line for a rune the game refuses is reworded for a player.

§1 — DEAD AIR AT 75%, MEASURED. “While no Mage stands, every hero deals 75% more damage.” Measured the way HP measured the other figures — the second difficulty, no talents, 150 runs for each of the same two parties: while it holds it multiplies the damage the three standing heroes deal by exactly 1.75; over an average normal fight it adds 35 damage in the first party and 18 in the second, about 0.6 and 0.35 of what a flat 10% on every hero would add; and it changed neither how often heroes fell nor how deep a run went. It is still the least of the three crest runes over a whole fight, because no Mage stands for only about a tenth of one — which is how often the condition holds, not what the rune pays when it does. A core rune held by a standing hero is now asked at every fall and every raise: a rune paid while a hero carries, say, Pack Bond stops the moment its only holder falls and starts again when he is raised, and the battle log says so both ways. This took more than the one line it was priced at — the battle's re-reading of who stands had never carried the heroes' core runes, so without them the question could only answer no. Breaking Heals a Hero now reads “An attack that lands Break heals the lowest-health hero for 20% of it.” — it said every point of Break damage dealt, and it has only ever paid for Break an attack lands. Its rule is not widened; that is still owed as a talent rebalance. A rune the game refuses can reach a player — a modified data file or an old save can carry one — so its line in the battle log now reads “it cannot work as written, so it pays nothing this fight”, and the technical reason goes to the error log.

§2 — THE WORD THE TAXONOMY WAS MISSING. Every rune has a type — ability, passive or stat — and may have a secondary label saying what gates it. The only one in use was tradeoff. Conditional joins it: a conditional rune's effect pays only while its condition holds, and so it pays more than a rune that always pays. Empty Pulpit, Dead Air and Dirge carry it; every rune in the file was checked, retired ones included, and no other rune carries a condition of any kind. A check now holds the label and the condition together in both directions, so a rune written with a condition and no label, or a label and no condition, is caught. The glossary's rune entry says it in a player's words.

§3 — TWO CHECKS THAT READ THE WRONG DOOR. Two checks print how often each rune is offered at the Peddler, a cache and a bargain, and their Peddler column had been counting the cache's roll since the Peddler got a door of its own — one that never sells a core rune. Both now count the Peddler's own door. Every Peddler figure but one moved, no cache or bargain figure did, and the figures moved the way the arithmetic says: the Peddler draws from the same list less its core runes, so each rune in it comes up a little more often. Both checks now also confirm the column is the Peddler's, by finding no core rune in it where the cache beside it offers some. A check that walked every rune through the shared rule and called that proof the whole pool “still applies” now says only what it shows — that no rune breaks the rule outright — and names the checks that test what each rune actually pays.

§4 — HOUSEKEEPING. HP's spent test folders were moved to the Trash under a folder that names them. The 461 older ones are the designer's and were not touched, nor the save backups.

§5 — DELIBERATELY NOT DONE. Whether a fallen hero stays down between fights is still open, and The Cairn of the Fallen and a Revive Potion used from the map still wait on it. The Skirmisher and the Tracker are still ruled and not built, and their new names are still owed. Tithe's rule is not widened. No rune was authored, and no figure moved but Dead Air's. The class-count question still names one class. No crest rune grants a card. The crest is still one slot. Volley, Ambusher and Channel's tempo payout are still their own batch, and the rules file is not split.

The checks that moved, and why. Every existing check was first run as it stood against this batch's game, with the documents as HP left them — 131 targets, in 75 minutes — and two read red. The check HP's rulings were written against read red on exactly the eight lines predicted: five questions read as the fight runs where four stood, the core-rune question no longer read once, a payload asking it refused, and the three crest runes' new label. One was not predicted: the talent-tree check that holds every talent's text to the number its effect pays could not find for 20% of its value in Breaking Heals a Hero's new words, so it now looks for the number in the new ones. Every other check read exactly what it read at HP, the two Peddler tables included, line for line — so nothing this batch changed in the game moved them, and re-pointing them is the whole of their movement. Six checks were re-pointed, each to what it was written to ask, with its reason at the place: the HP check (twenty-three more checks — the five questions, the cycle round a Pack Bond holder's fall and rise, the refused line's words and the route that lets it reach a player, the three figures at the ruling and their new label, the talent's words, and the records), the rune-condition check (three more: the new label agrees with every rune's condition, both ways), the two Peddler checks (nine and four more: each column provably the Peddler's), the talent-tree check (its words) and the whole-pool walk (its message). Eighteen controls, one defect each, were read by what each printed, and every one went red on the line it aimed at — among them the brief's one line on its own, which leaves a crest asking about a core rune never paying, and the game's Peddler selling core runes, which the old Peddler columns read green. Four rows of the count table move and three more carry a note. The full rehearsal run, in a copy proved equal to the tree, read every one of the 131 checks at the count written down for it before it started, and the count differ found nothing to report.

2026-09-30 — Batch HP: THE CONDITION AS A FIGHT RUNS, AND THE THREE RUNES IT MAKES REAL

A crest rune can now ask who is standing while a fight runs, not only as it opens — and three crest runes do: Empty Pulpit (while no Cleric stands, every hero takes less damage), Dead Air (while no Mage stands, every hero deals more) and Dirge (while a hero lies fallen, the rest deal more). Each switches on the moment its hero falls and off the moment he is raised, and the battle log says so both ways. Fellowship is retired, Tithe's words say what it actually pays, and three small omissions in the run summary are fixed. Full working, and the rulings this batch needs: docs/reports/HP.md.

§0 — HO'S SEVEN RULINGS. All seven are taken. The three runes HO held back are built on a condition read as the fight runs — chosen over letting a roster leave a class out, keeping a fallen hero down between fights, or leaving them unbuilt, because it makes all three real and turns each into a moment in a fight rather than a choice made in the lobby. Tithe stays at 30%; its words narrow. Fellowship is retired. FN's old ruling — that a rune with a condition at the flat price is strictly worse than one without — stands, and is answered by the figure: a conditioned rune pays more than the bare version would. A card naming its own cost or its own payout is confirmed as outside the rule against naming a number a constant holds. The crest's words on the Peddler's row, a cache's button, the event's line and the toast are kept, with no marker on a crest rune's name. And two of HO's names are changed before they were ever in the game: Cold Hearth (beside Cold Iron, Cold Snap, Deep Cold and Killing Cold) is Dead Air, and Gravesong (beside the Grave Totem and two Grave- relics) is Dirge. Tithe keeps its name.

§1 — A CONDITION ON THE FOUR IS READ AS THE FIGHT RUNS. Until now a crest rune's condition was checked once, as a fight opened. But every fight a run plays forward opens with one hero of each class, all standing — the draft seats one of each, and a hero who falls in a fight that is won stands again at its end — so a rune about a missing Cleric or a fallen hero could only ever pay a player who quit a fight and came back. Now four of the five questions a crest can ask — is a class standing, is a class missing, how many of a class stand, is everyone standing — are asked again every time a hero falls and every time one is raised. Those are the only two moments a hero's standing can change: the game marks a hero down in one place and up in one place, and both now tell the battle. The fifth question, whether a hero carries a given core rune, is still asked once, as the fight opens. The number a rune adds comes back exactly. A rune that switches off is not subtracted; the battle keeps every hero's figure from before any such rune touched it and rebuilds it, so a hero who opens at 10% more damage from a relic is back at exactly 10% when the rune stops — taking 25% back off 35% would have left him a hair under. What such a rune may change is limited to what the game re-reads on every blow — damage dealt and taken, parry, Block, armor piercing, and the talent-tree effects read the same way. Anything the game builds once — maximum health, the resource bar, Attack after a fight's scaling, a stat a companion copies from its Hunter when he calls it — cannot be taken back mid-fight, so a rune that tried would be refused as the game loads it and pays nothing. A fight quit after a hero fell and resumed opens with him down, and a rune that pays while a hero is down pays from its opening, as it should.

§2 — EMPTY PULPIT, DEAD AIR AND DIRGE. “While no Cleric stands, every hero takes 50% less damage.” “While no Mage stands, every hero deals 50% more damage.” “While a hero lies fallen, the rest deal 45% more damage.” Crest runes at 150 gold, reached as any rune is — a drop, the Peddler, a cache — and worn in the crest. Every figure is a proposal, and each was measured first. At the second difficulty with no talents, over two different parties and 150 runs each: a normal fight lasts six to eight rounds; a hero is down for about a fifth of it; the first fall of a fight that has one comes about a third of the way in; the Cleric is down for 2–6% of it and the Mage for 7–15%, depending on which Mage; and a hero is almost never revived by the game's own play. At the figures the brief proposed — 12%, 12% and 25% — the three would have added under 1% to 5% of a fight's damage, and none moved how far a run got. At the figures above, each pays four and a half to five times what the same effect on a bare rune would while it holds; over an average normal fight Dirge is worth 72–86% of what a flat 10% on every hero would add to the same fights, Empty Pulpit about four fifths, and Dead Air a quarter to a half — the one to watch, because how often no Mage stands depends on which Mage the party has. Dirge was the only one to move how deep a run went, and only in the stronger party.

§3 — FELLOWSHIP IS RETIRED. It cleared a debuff from an ally on every hero's turn. The heroes carry about 0.3 debuffs a round, so four cleanses a round was thirteen times what was ever needed at any setting, and the whole layer it replaced — the Cleric's Unburden, the Returned Burden rune, two talents, the Medic core rune and four cards — was worth about 2% of damage taken. No number could fix that shape. It is no longer offered anywhere. Like every retired rune it is kept: a saved run already wearing one still has it and it still works, though no save the designer holds carries it.

§4 — TITHE SAYS WHAT IT PAYS. It now reads “A hero's attack that lands Break heals whoever among the four is lowest, for 30% of it.” It always read only the Break an ordinary attack actually lands — not a card's own special Break, a trap, a companion, the Long Watch's carry, or a blow into an enemy already Broken — where its words promised every hero's Break damage. The rule it pays through is also the talent Breaking Heals a Hero's, which every class can buy, so it was not widened: that is a talent rebalance with its own measurement, and it is recorded as owed.

§5 — A RUNE WITH A CONDITION PAYS MORE THAN ONE WITHOUT. Three checks refused any rune carrying a condition, each for its own old reason. Each now asks its own question: one that no rune reads a talent node, one that no rune brings back the retired card-tag conditions, and one — the rule itself — that a rune carrying a condition is authored at a figure beyond the one the same effect carries without, so a conditioned rune written at a bare rune's figure is caught.

§6 — THE SMALL THINGS. The run summary at the end of a run now names each hero's core runes, what the crest wore and what was left in the bag — three things it had left out since each was built. The simulation's shop no longer stocks a core rune, which the real Peddler has not sold for three batches. A re-roll in the Peddler's stock that could never run is gone, from the game and from the simulation's copy of it. Thirteen comments in the code that stated Faith's release threshold as a number now name the rule instead — the five the last batch listed and eight more found by looking. And the last batch's fifteen spent test folders were moved to the Trash, under a folder that names them.

§7 — DELIBERATELY NOT DONE. Whether a hero who falls stays down between fights is still open: it is a question of how hard a run is, and the three runes no longer depend on it — the event The Cairn of the Fallen and a Revive Potion used from the map still wait on it. The Skirmisher and the Tracker were ruled into ordinary runes in conversation and that is recorded as ruled and not built, with new names owed. No counting of any class, no either-or condition, no crest rune that grants a card, no Kinship rune, no crest for a missing Warrior or Hunter, Tithe's rule not widened, the crest still one slot, the rules file not split, and Volley, Ambusher and Channel's tempo payout still their own batch.

The checks that moved, and why. Every existing check was first run as it stood against this batch's game and documents — 129 of them, in 74 minutes — and thirteen read red, every one a check asserting something this batch was ruled to change: the rune file's size and the crest's count, a table of rune tags three rows longer than it expected, checks that took any condition on a rune for the card-tag conditions FN retired, fixtures that paid maximum health through a condition the fight now re-reads — which the new door refuses, because maximum health is built once — Fellowship live and offered, and the three held-back runes absent from the file. Thirteen checks were re-pointed, each to what it was written to ask, with its reason at the place, and one more lists the new check among those that read a card's tag. One check predicted to go red did not: it already counted crest runes by name. Six read green with a higher count, each walking the three new runes and one fewer Fellowship — and one of them, the check that drives a seeded road, is offered a different set of cards because every offer now holds four crest runes. Run again with the three set aside and Fellowship restored, it read exactly what it read before HP, message for message. The same trial answered the brief's question about two tables of shop tallies: they moved for the same reason, not because of the simulation's counter, which neither of them reads. A new check, check_hp, drives this batch (145 checks): the four keys at both doors, the two field lists, the refusal, the reversal round a full cycle, a fight quit and resumed, the three runes through a real drop, a real fight, a fall and a revive, Fellowship retired with a save still wearing it, Tithe's words and the small things. Thirty-eight controls, one defect each, were read by what each printed. Two were rebuilt before they were trusted — one broke the file it was aimed at instead of the rule, and one took a rune's figure off in a way that comes back exact on one of the two bases it was aimed at — and one re-pointed check was split so that each of its defects names itself. The full rehearsal run found one thing more, in the game's own words: five of the new refusal reasons said party-wide, a word retired from anything a player might read, and the check that sweeps every string in the game's scripts caught them. They say a stamp across the four now. Fourteen rows of the count table move, one of them new, and nine more carry a note.

2026-09-30 — Batch HO: THE SUPPLY ROUTE, THE TRAP, AND THE FIRST CREST RUNES

Two crest runes exist — Tithe and Fellowship — and they reach the player through every door a rune already comes through; nothing had to be built for that, but three screens offered one in a single hero's name and now say it is for the crest. Three more crest runes were specified and are not in the game, because in a run played forward none of the three could ever pay. The trap HN's census found is closed: an effect that adds to a stat now adds to it, where it used to replace it. Elevation's card carries the ruled words. Full working, and the rulings this batch needs: docs/reports/HO.md.

§0 — HN'S THREE RULINGS. Forbearance keeps its bare name. The long form, Rune of Forbearance, was an error in HN's brief and never an option that was weighed and turned down: the game has not held that name at any point, so nothing was reversed. Elevation's card reads as ruled: “Raise them up: every ally gains 2 stacks of Faith. An ally who reaches the cap with it RELEASES on the spot — and their peak does not fall for it.” The comments on its handler say it the same way, and a standing rule is recorded from it: card text and comments do not name a magnitude a constant holds — they say it relative to the rule (reaches the cap, the threshold) or not at all, so the next time a number moves there is no second copy of it left behind. And the replace-not-add trap is closed. Every hero now opens a fight carrying each of his stats' starting values before any rune, talent or crest is applied. So an effect that adds 20% healing received gives a Warrior 120% where it used to leave him 20%; the Cleric's Holy Conduit adds its 15% to whatever he has (135% under that same effect) where it used to set the total; and a hero's parry chance starts from his own baseline — 5%, or the Swordmaster's 12% — before anything adds to it. Eighteen stats have a starting value that is not zero; the heroes already carried seven and now carry all eighteen, read off the unit's own list so that one added later is carried without anybody remembering to. Nothing in the live game wrote one of the eleven that were missing, so no live number moved.

What it changes on a saved run — one magnitude, in two runes. Four retired runes change healing received, and a saved run can still be wearing one. Two of them were broken by the trap. The Vampiric Rune (“heals received are 30% weaker”) on a Warrior, a Mage or a Hunter replaced the hero's 100% with −30%, which the game reads as no healing at all, and the Rune of the Killing Cold (“25% weaker”, a Mage's) did the same with −25%. Measured on a save carrying each, before and after: a heal of 40 landed 0. It now lands 28 under the Vampiric Rune (100% − 30% = 70%) and 30 under the Killing Cold (100% − 25% = 75%), which is what each rune's words have always said. The other two are the Cleric's, whose Holy Conduit had already given him a value to add to, and they read as they did: the Hollow Chalice 85% (115% − 30%) and the Martyr 130% (115% + 15%), and a Cleric wearing the Vampiric Rune 85%. The player's own save holds none of the four.

§1 — HOW A CREST RUNE REACHES THE PLAYER. Every door already offered one, so nothing was built and the authoring below was not held up. A crest rune belongs to no class, and the one test every roll applies passes it for every hero. So it drops after a normal fight (it is one rune among everything the four could be offered, counted once); the Peddler can have it on his counter, once, whichever hero it was rolled against; an elite's cache and a bargain's rune reward can hold it; and the event that grants a rune can grant it. Taken, it is worn in the Crest when that was asked for and the Crest is free, and otherwise goes into the bag — it never sits on a hero. What was wrong was the words. Each of those rolls asks one hero at a time, so three screens offered a crest rune in that hero's name: the Peddler's row said (for Warrior 1), a cache's button gave the bare name among that hero's three, and the event's line named the hero who took it. Each now says for the crest, as the victory card, the full-bag panel and the Peddler's sell rows already did; and a crest rune picked from a cache with the Crest empty is worn at once and the map says so — “Tithe fills the crest — every hero wears it.” (proposed words). The simulation's own shop, which keeps a counter of its own, could stock one crest rune for several heroes; it stocks it once.

§2 — EVERY HERO STANDING: FALSE MEANS SOMETHING NOW. HN found that a crest condition written that way was ignored. It now means a hero is down as the fight opens, and true still means all four stand. It is read once, as the fight opens, and a hero raised during the fight does not switch it — accepted only because a rune that uses it must say when, in its own words: as the fight opens, enter a fight. Driven both ways against a party of four standing and a party with one down. No rune in the game uses it (§3).

§3 — THE CREST RUNES: TWO AUTHORED, THREE NOT. Tithe: “Every hero's Break damage heals whoever among the four is lowest, for 30% of its value.” Fellowship: “At the start of each hero's turn, he clears one debuff from an ally.” Each costs 150 gold like every rune, belongs to no class, wears no marker on its name (proposed, as the brief proposed) and is one entry among the rest wherever a rune is rolled. Each pays through the rule a talent already uses — Breaking Heals a Hero and Cleanse Debuffs Each Turn — and is added to it for a hero who has both. The brief expected no new code for that, and there is some: a rune may not write a talent's own counter (the rune charter, EM), so each writes a counter of its own that is summed where the talent's is read, and the combat log names the rune when the rune is what paid.

Tithe's number was measured before it was chosen, and it is proposed, not settled. In normal fights in the second zone (difficulty 2, no talents), a party with no Break specialist books about 47 Break damage a round, and a party whose Warrior runs the Break lane — the Long Watch and Bared Plate runes, which is what the measurement found that lane to be — about 69. Tithe pays on less than either, and the same for both: about 41 a round, because it reads only the Break an ordinary blow applies as it lands — none into an enemy already Broken, and nothing for Break dealt by a card's own effect, a trap, a companion or the Long Watch's carry onto a second enemy. At 30% that is about 12 health a round to the lowest hero, about 6% of that hero's health bar, and a little over 90 health a fight. With it worn from the first fight, heroes fell 0.38 times a normal fight in the first zone where they fell 0.54 without it, and ended those fights at 83% of their health where they ended at 72%; fights did not get shorter or longer. 20% was too small to see in deaths (0.50) and 40% cut them by more than half (0.23). About half of what it heals replaces healing the Cleric or a potion would have done. A hero who already has the talent's 20% gains less from the rune than these figures say.

Fellowship ships at 1, and at 1 it is not too strong as power and is total as coverage. Enemies land about 0.3 debuffs on the heroes a round; four heroes each clearing one a turn is thirteen times that. So with Fellowship 96% of every debuff that lands is cleared, 99% of them before the hero carrying it next acts. In ordinary fights what that removes is small — about 2% of the damage the heroes take, and under half an action a fight spent Crippled — and no outcome moved outside its error: damage taken, deaths, or how long fights last. It matters most against the Withered Warden, whose area poison is about an eighth of that fight's damage. What it costs elsewhere: the Cleric's Unburden has almost nothing left to lift (0.45 casts a fight falls to 0.01), and the Returned Burden rune is starved with it; beside the talent that cleanses twice a turn the rune adds nothing at all; and an enemy effect authored later that stuns or freezes a hero would be cleared before it cost him his turn about nineteen times in twenty — none can reach a hero today. The two builds the design conversation expected it to press on, the Occultist's Madness and the Hunter's breadth, count debuffs on enemies and do not feel it.

The three that were not authored are Empty Pulpit (no Cleric among the heroes: 12% less damage taken), Cold Hearth (no Mage: 12% more damage) and Gravesong (enter a fight with a hero down: 25% more damage). None of the three could pay in any fight a run played forward opens. The draft seats exactly one Warrior, one Mage, one Cleric and one Hunter — four picks from a roster of four — and a hero who falls in a fight that is won stands again, at a fifth of his health, when it ends. The one state in which each would pay is a fight quit after a hero fell and then resumed, so each would pay a player for quitting and nobody else. A rune that ships and cannot pay is worse than one that does not ship, and a batch may not re-aim a rune on its own judgment, so the three are held back: what each would pay is measured (12% less damage taken is about a sixth of what a Cleric supplies), and what it would take to make each one real is priced in the report for a ruling. Both reasons are held as checks that go red, saying the runes can be authored, the day the roster grows or the fallen stay down. The no-Warrior and no-Hunter crests were cut in the brief, and the reason is recorded with these: the same draft rules them out.

The names. All five were swept against every name the game holds — 1,019 of them: abilities, enemies and their abilities, glossary terms, items, talents, relics, runes (retired ones included), statuses and tags — with a near-miss counted as a hit. Fellowship and Empty Pulpit meet nothing. Tithe meets the relic Tithing Scales, whose internal id is the same word in a different table, and a combat-log line, Blood Tithe, left by a talent that no longer exists and that nothing can trigger. Gravesong shares its first syllable with the enemy Grave Totem and the relics Gravelight Lantern and Gravewrought Coin. Cold Hearth shares a word with the card Cold Iron and the runes Cold Snap, Deep Cold and Killing Cold (and the retired Rune of the Killing Cold). None is the same name and none was renamed: they are reported for the designer to rule on.

§4 — A TEST THAT FAILED ABOUT ONE RUN IN FIFTY-FOUR IS REPAIRED. One check of the blacksmith bought the first upgrade on the counter and then asked whether that kind of upgrade could still be offered for a different card. When the first upgrade was one that fits only a single card the four heroes hold — Widened, which here fits Magic Missiles alone — there was no other card, and the check failed for a reason that had nothing to do with the rule it guards. It now buys the first upgrade whose kind has another card to go on, and says so if the counter holds none. That was chosen over fixing the dice, because a fixed seed would have kept the test passing without ever meeting the counter that broke it. The check is no looser: it asks the same question of a counter that can answer it.

§5 — SPENT TEST COPIES ARE CLEARED BY THE NEXT BATCH. Each batch tests in isolated copies of the game, and each copy leaves a small folder of its own beside the player's saves. The rule is now recorded: a batch clears the previous batch's folders when it finishes, never its own, which it may still need, and never the player's own folder or the save backups. HN's eighteen were moved to the Trash under a folder that names them. Before: 479 spent folders (461 older ones, 138.9 MiB, and HN's eighteen, 1.4 MiB). After: the 461 older ones, which the policy does not reach and which were left where they are, and this batch's own. The 1.4 MiB comes back when the Trash is emptied, which is the designer's to do.

No any class form for counting a class among the heroes, and no either-or form for a condition; no crest rune that grants a card; no Kinship rune; no crest for a missing Warrior or Hunter; the half of the crest census that would read the heroes as a fight runs is not built; the Crest still holds one rune; the rules file was not split; and the Volley, Ambusher and Channel tempo ideas were not built.

Five comments in the code that said no crest rune exists say what is true. Not changed: the simulation's shop can still stock a core rune, which the real Peddler has not sold since HL.
