§1 — THE DRAFT CARD GETS THE COMPUTED BLOCK, AND IT IS ONE BUILDER FOR TWO SCREENS

The draft card rendered ab.description and nothing else — no computed damage, no Break damage, no cost, no cooldown, no initiative and no Perfect — on the one screen whose whole purpose is comparing cards. It now renders all of them, under the description, in a second label and a cooler tint so authored prose and computed figures read as different kinds of writing.

WHAT THIS FIXES. 30 of the 66 damaging abilities never state their damage in prose, because every other surface computes it. Ice Lance was the sharpest case: damage: 35 in the dict against a card reading only "+5% of Attack per Chilled stack", so it drafted as a per-stack ability. Aimed Shot's card was, in full, "A perfect line. Patient, precise, final." — damage: 45, perfect_text: "+20 Focus", a blank card for the hardest-hitting entry in the Sharpshooter's pool. And all 190-odd perfect_text strings were invisible while drafting.

THE BRIEF SAID TO REUSE THE BLOCK blacksmith_screen.gd ALREADY BUILDS, AND THAT BLOCK DOES NOT EXIST. The claim came forward from CJ's own report and neither this batch nor CJ had checked it: blacksmith_screen.gd renders Run.upgrade_desc and ab.description and no computed values at all. The only two computed blocks in the game were battle._ability_tooltip and an inline one in party_screen. So the shared builder had to be WRITTEN rather than reused — Classes.computed_block — and BOTH the draft card and the hero sheet were pointed at it, which is what keeps the copy count at two instead of taking it to three. That was the whole point of the instruction, and it is honoured; the premise behind it was simply wrong.

THE THIRD COPY IS DELIBERATELY LEFT ALONE AND THE REASON IS RECORDED. battle._ability_tooltip reads a live BattleUnit: Surge, Empower and the Resonance curve multiply its damage and it prints "(ready in N)" off that unit's cooldown clock. It is a mid-combat tooltip with live state in it rather than a static card, and folding it in would move numbers inside a fight.

THE CARD SHOWS THE SCALING PERCENTAGE, NOT A COMPUTED RANGE, AND THAT IS A DECISION WITH A REASON. The map screen has no live Attack to compute a range from. Only party_screen._draw_detail and the battle spawn build a hero's live Attack, each with its own sixty-line prologue (hero config, kit overrides, passive, spec stats, tree, runes, upgrades, node scaling) — and a third copy of THAT on the map screen would be a far worse duplication than the one this section exists to prevent. The shared builder takes an Attack figure and prints the real range when it is given one, so the hero sheet gets the range and the draft card gets the percentage, and when that prologue is extracted the draft card changes by one argument. Recorded as owed in CLAUDE.md. Showing the percentage is not a workaround: the draft screen is one of the two arithmetic-ALLOWED surfaces in the standard, which may show percentages and formulas outright.

THE HERO SHEET GAINED THREE LINES AND LOST NONE. Routing it through the shared builder gives it the cost, cooldown and initiative lines the block carries — every one a number that sheet already had and was not showing. Its "Scaling: N% of Attack" line stays outside the block, because on the draft card the block's own damage line IS the scaling and the card would print it twice.

THE LAYOUT RISK WAS REAL AND IT IS REPORTED RATHER THAN PAPERED OVER, EXACTLY AS THE BRIEF ASKED. Measured in a headless run of the real screen with the real theme font (Open Sans SemiBold), across three sampled draws:

§1 — BERSERKER: the ratchet, the band, the payout

These are the first cards in the game that read frenzy_floor at all. Blood Frenzy has two halves — a live bonus that scales with missing health, and a FLOOR that keeps half the peak he reaches — and until now nothing anywhere had a lever on the second.

  • UNSLAKED (30 Rage, 2.0, 5cd, self) — for 3 turns (Perfect: 4) the floor captures the FULL bonus he reaches rather than half. The play pattern is the point: cast it, then dive with Blood Offering, and the floor locks in at double its usual value for the rest of the battle. It lives inside frenzy_bonus(), the ONE place the floor is decided, so the strike multiplier, the nameplate readout and the spec chip all answer it for free. It uses maxf rather than an assignment, so a Scar Tissue Berserker already keeping 100% is never quietly lowered to it — the card can only ever raise what is kept.
  • SPITE (20 Rage, 1.5, 4cd, self) — for 4 turns (Perfect: 6) he takes 1% less damage per 5% of maximum health missing, up to 30% (Perfect: 40%). MITIGATION, NOT HEALING, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE: a card that healed him here would fight his own passive, taking back the missing health Blood Frenzy pays for, so it would cancel itself. This keeps him inside the frenzy band instead of lifting him out of it.
  • BOIL OVER (40 Rage, 2.5, 5cd, one enemy, 15 BD) — one strike for 30% of Attack plus 2% more per POINT of his live Blood Frenzy bonus. For 2 turns afterwards (Perfect: 1) he receives only the FLOOR, not the live bonus. The floor itself is untouched, and the ratchet still runs inside the window — which is exactly what makes Unslaked pair with it: a high floor makes the recovery cost small.

THE ORDERING IS LOAD-BEARING AND IS THE THING THIS CARD COULD MOST EASILY GET WRONG. Boil Over's per-point term is read at the raw-damage block, ABOVE the strike; the recovery status is applied AFTER the strike loop. Applied first, the term would have read the floor it was about to impose — the card quietly paying a fraction of what it promises, with nothing to announce it. It also draws no variance roll of its own: raw already carries one, and a second randf_range would shift every later roll in the battle (the draw-order gotcha Batch AQ spent a bisect on).

§2 — WARDEN: deny the reset, get paid for it, or turn it outward

Heavy Plating climbs +8% Block per unblocked hit and a BLOCK throws the whole climb away. That sawtooth is the passive's central cruelty and nothing had ever answered it. These are three answers, and two of them are deliberately incompatible.

  • ANVIL (25 Rage, 2.0, 5cd, self) — for 3 turns (Perfect: 4), Blocking does NOT reset the Heavy Plating bonus. The sawtooth becomes a staircase. FLAGGED, NOT TUNED: with the bonus free to climb to its +40% ceiling and stay there, this is potentially the strongest card in the batch. It ships as written and play prices it.
  • RECOMPENSE (20 Rage, 1.5, 4cd, self) — for 4 turns (Perfect: 6), whenever Blocking resets his Heavy Plating bonus he gains Rage equal to the percentage points lost: a reset from +32% pays 32 Rage. The figure is read BEFORE the reset, in the unit the card advertises; read after the write it would always be zero, which is the class of mistake that reads exactly like the card doing nothing.
  • TURN THE BLADE (25 Rage, 2.0, 4cd, self) — for 3 turns (Perfect: 4), each Block deals Break damage to the attacker equal to half the damage the block refused. A blow that gets THROUGH pays nothing.

ANVIL AND RECOMPENSE ACTIVELY FIGHT EACH OTHER AND THAT IS INTENDED. Anvil prevents the reset; Recompense is paid by it. A Warden holding both gets nothing from the second while the first is up — and that falls out of the ordering rather than being a carve-out anybody has to remember: with no reset there is nothing to pay for. They are two answers to the same cruelty, not a stack, it has not been smoothed over, and both card descriptions say so outright rather than leaving a player to discover it.

§3 The structural changes — standing rules for EVERY gate

  • A GATE THAT RUNS ZERO CHECKS MUST FAIL. An empty gate is a broken gate, and it is the one case where silence has to be loud. A gate printing a count of zero is visibly broken; the same gate printing PASS is not.
  • A GATE MUST REACH ITS OWN END — a THIRD rule, added because the brief's two do not actually close the shape, and flagged rather than slipped in. §2 — The elite draft screen

Every living hero is offered a draft, and each draws their own three cards from their own spec pool and their own class pool at the established one-in-four seam. There is no shared offer and no shared ledger: Run.roll_draft_offer is called per member and reads that member's own pools and their own draft_refused, so a Pyromancer can never be shown a Warden card and one hero's decline can never hide a card from another. That ledger being per member is load-bearing — a shared refusal list is the obvious implementation and the wrong one, and it is verified rather than assumed.

ONE SCREEN, FOUR COLUMNS. Four sequential offers after every elite would slow a run noticeably, so the shape of the screen is the change and not just its reach. The rules carried over unaltered, each still owned by run_state.gd rather than by the screen:

  • Declining is per hero and independent. One hero taking a card does not commit the others.
  • A declined or dropped card enters that hero's no-return ledger and does not return that run — per member, never shared.
  • The seven-slot cap holds per column. A hero at the cap must name what the incoming card replaces before their column counts as decided, and only earned abilities can be named: protected ones are simply not in that list.
  • Offers fill short rather than padding with repeats (AP §3, unchanged). A hero with nothing left to be shown gets no column at all rather than an empty one.

THE SCREEN RESOLVES AS ONE ACTION, and that is the piece that decided the implementation. Every column is chosen, then the whole screen is confirmed — so a player who fills the Devout's last slot can still reconsider before the Warden's pick is locked. The choices are therefore staged, not committed: nothing touches the run until the confirm, which then walks the columns through Run.take_draft_ability and Run.decline_draft — the same two doors the boss pick's overlay already used. Leaving the screen commits nothing and the picks stay owed on the hero cards, so a player can go and read a hero sheet mid-draft. Clicking a staged card again unstages it, because declining costs a whole offer and must never be the only way out of a misclick.

PREPARATION'S THREE RULES, DECIDED HERE RATHER THAN BY WHICHEVER BRANCH THE CODE FELL INTO.

  • The extra turn is a FULL turn and it re-ticks his statuses. Designer's call: simpler and legible. It falls out of the implementation rather than being bolted on — he re-enters the ordinary initiative loop, so his damage-over-time effects bite, his buffs shorten and his cooldowns tick, exactly as on any turn. The cost is real without being punishing because ENEMY statuses tick on ENEMY turns, so the board he just armed is untouched by his second action.
  • It must not chain. Casting Preparation on the extra turn to earn another is an unbounded loop — a hang, not a balance question — so the cast is refused while one is pending. That refusal is load-bearing rather than tidy, and it is the negative control test_batch_bv drives directly.
  • The delay is what makes it a setup card rather than "take two turns now". A counter armed at 2 and decremented at the end of each of his turns is what makes that exact: the turn he cast on burns one and grants nothing, and his next turn grants the extra one.

THE Improvised INTERACTION IS REPORTED, NOT PRE-TUNED — AND THE MEASUREMENT DISAGREED WITH THE BRIEF. Two standing rules, set once

1. Charges and the effects that ride them count HITS, not casts. A multi-hit ability spends one charge per hit and fires its on-hit effects per hit. Aimed Volley is three shots; under Arcane Arrows it spends three of five charges and forks three times. Magic Missiles and Called Volley behave the same way. This is a real power increase for multi-hit abilities and it is deliberate — it is what makes a multi-hit kit and a charge bank a build rather than a coincidence.

Applied retroactively, and here is what actually changed. The rule was audited across every charge bank in the game:

  • Mirror Image (Batch BQ) now spends one image per hit of a multi-hit attack. The gate is multi_hits alone, which is what keeps the card's own promise true: a multi-hit ability is repeated strikes on ONE target, i.e. single-target, while an area attack, a random scatter and a chosen pair are not and still spend nothing. REPORTED: this changes nothing in play today. No enemy in enemies.json carries a multi_hits or random_hits ability, and heroes do not attack the Mage — so it is the rule made TRUE ahead of its use rather than a behaviour change, and the day an enemy gains a multi-hit blow it is already right. Asserted, not assumed: test_batch_br greps the roster for both fields.
  • Interpose's shield_charges, Waiting Guard's banked_guards and Feint's feint_guards ALREADY counted hits — verified at their sites rather than assumed. The block branch and the parry roll both sit inside the strike loop, so a three-hit blow already spent three. No change; the suite pins the positions.
  • ONE PLACE THE RULE WAS DELIBERATELY NOT APPLIED, and it is reported rather than buried: SPRAY OF ARROWS. Batch AZ gates it ab.multi_hits == 0 and ab.random_hits == 0 by design — spray is how many extra ENEMIES a single shot finds, and a multi-hit ability already finds its target three times. Firing it per hit would triple a shipped talent's magnitude, which is a balance change this batch's standing testing scope forbids measuring. The gate is pinned as a finding so a later batch has to read the reasoning first. The same shape applies to the Survivalist's post-loop on-hit package (Coated Blades, Venom Coating): it fires once per cast, and moving it inside the loop would both triple its output and change whether a MISSED or BLOCKED strike still applies it — a second, unasked change riding along.

The Warrior recovery card was authored as Second Wind, which Holy already holds (tranche 1, Batch BO), and is Battle Trance here. That one was a true duplicate and had to be renamed: the resolver is keyed on display_name, so two abilities sharing one would make pool_ability answer the wrong question.

Iron Will's Break half is a CAP, not an immunity — decided, not left open

It is cleared at every tick (and at the cast), which is what makes "since his last turn" true rather than "since the trance began". The arriving-stance principle, first, because it is the standing rule the batch leaves behind

The Swordmaster's stances were a binary toggle with passive numbers on each side, and nothing in his kit had ever behaved differently depending on which one he was in. Both of his new cards read the stance and then switch it — and the thing that makes the switch a feature rather than a tax is the rule they are both authored against:

Each branch buys what the stance he is ARRIVING in wants. Cast from Aggressive he lands in Defensive, so the ability hands him defence. Cast from Defensive he lands in Aggressive, so it hands him offence. He is never stranded — he always arrives holding something. This is now a standing design rule, recorded because it is the thing a later stance ability would most easily get backwards. Stance-GATED abilities — ones that require a stance to cast rather than branching on it — are a noted future direction and were deliberately not built here.

The pivot itself is one implementation with three callers now (_swordmaster_switch): Guard Change, Precision Strike and Feint. Tempo is part of the pivot rather than part of Guard Change, and that is a decision rather than a convenience — the node's own text reads "switching stance grants +30% damage for 1 turn" and names no ability, so a swap that skipped it would make a shipped tooltip false. Everything Guard Change pays beyond the pivot — its Break damage, Sunder Guard, No Quarter, the parry perfect — stays on Guard Change, because those are on its card and no other.

The six

Berserker — how do you get low, and what do you do with the blood. His damage scales with missing health and his Bloodletting lane is built entirely on bleedout events — and he controlled neither. Enemy damage decided when he reached his power band; a 100-point buildup decided when four of his own nodes fired.

  • Blood Offering — 0 Rage, 1.5 initiative, 3cd, self. He loses 20% of his CURRENT health and gains 40 Rage (Perfect: 60). Axis: buying the frenzy band on purpose. Percent of current, not maximum, is the whole safety argument: it can never take him below 1, and its absolute cost shrinks as he drops, which is correct for a spec that wants to live low rather than die low. The health is removed directly rather than through the damage path — this is a price he pays, not a wound anyone dealt, and routing it through take_hit would have fed Blood Price, the recap's damage-taken ledger and every on-damage rider with an event that had no attacker.
  • Gut Rip — 30 Rage, 2.5, 4cd, one enemy. Bleeds the target out at once whatever its buildup, and deals 6% of Attack for every 10 points consumed (Perfect: 9%). Axis: the bleedout stops being the enemy's clock. It fires the REAL bleedout path — the meter is topped up to 100 through _add_bleed_with_burst rather than a private copy of the burst being written into the ability — so Bloodcraze, Scent of Blood, Arterial Spray and Blood Tithe all answer it, the Exsanguination capstone and the Rune of Exsanguination still apply, and Slaughterhouse still leaves the meter at 50 rather than 0, which means a second Gut Rip four turns later has half a wound waiting for it. Swordmaster — what is a stance worth.

  • Precision Strike — 20 Rage, 2.0, 3cd, one enemy, then switches stance. From Aggressive: strikes twice for 20% of Attack each and grants +25% parry chance for 3 turns. From Defensive: strikes once for 15% of Attack with 15 Break damage, and his attacks bypass all armor for 3 turns. Perfect: the stance it grants holds 4 turns. Axis: the same blade, two intentions. Armor is bypassed outright rather than penetrated by a percentage — armor penetration in this game is a fraction of the target's armor, so a percentage clause would have been "+50% of zero" against every unarmoured enemy in the game: a clause that silently does nothing, which is the exact dud the upgrade pool's eligibility rule exists to prevent. It reuses the existing bypass hook that Held Breath and Through and Through already ride.
  • Feint — 25 Rage, 2.0, 4cd, one enemy, then switches stance. From Aggressive: strikes for 35% of Attack, and the enemy's next attack is redirected at one of its own allies. From Defensive: no strike at all — he gains 2 charges (Perfect: 3), each of which parries an attack against him outright and deals its damage to the attacker instead. Axis: their swing lands somewhere they did not intend. Charges, not turns: both halves wait until spent, so a Feint cast into a lull is not wasted and cannot be dodged by an enemy simply not attacking that turn.

Warden — what does protection look like when it is not his own. Block is his signature stat and it had only ever protected him; Taunt brought damage to him one enemy at a time.

A party-wide defensive cooldown that costs him everything, and it is self-balancing — a bigger field means more mitigation as well as more incoming.
